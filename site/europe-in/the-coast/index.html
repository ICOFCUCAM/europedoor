Europe's coastlines

Europe has more coast per square kilometre than any other continent, and almost none of it looks like the postcard. Atlantic, Mediterranean, Adriatic, Baltic, Aegean, Black Sea and Arctic.

Themes that run through it

Theme

Viking Europe

The Norse were traders and settlers more often than raiders, and the archaeology says so: ships built for cargo, parliaments held in the open air, and towns founded from Dublin to Kyiv. These are the places where you can see the evidence rather than the reputation.

Theme

Roman Europe

Rome's best surviving buildings are mostly not in Rome. They are in the provinces, where a later city did not need the stone, or where a cathedral was built inside the temple instead of on top of it. These are the places where the engineering is still doing its job.

Theme

Island Europe

An island changes the pace before you arrive, because you have to wait for a boat. These eight are chosen for being genuinely themselves rather than resort annexes — a Baltic sand spit, a Hebridean distillery island, an Adriatic island closed to outsiders until 1989, and a Cycladic island that farms.
