The wall between editorial and commerce

Paid placement buys a declared slot. It never buys Atlas ranking, Journey Planner weighting, a place in a curated journey, a destination’s score, a search result’s order, or one word of editorial copy.

Declared slots on editorial surfaces, and the directory. Every one of them is off. This wall moved on 2026-09-18, and this page is where it moved: it used to read Paid tiers buy presentation on directory surfaces. What did not move is the substance — ranking, weighting, curation, scores, result order and copy were never for sale and are not now.

What a paid placement may never do

An advertiser may not buy an ordering. §8 says so and this is that sentence as a data structure: there is no field on a campaign a ranking could read, so no code path can be written that reads one. The same five words have been refused on every place and experience record in data/countries/ since before this brief arrived, in the schema and again at the file level.

There is no field on a campaign a ranking could read, so no code path can be written that reads one: rank, boost, weight, score, priority, relevance, featured_in_planner, recommendation, guide_weight are refused by name in the validator and again in the checks. A creative image is refused too, and for the register rather than for taste — nothing ships here without a photographer, a source, a licence, a date and the hash of the bytes, and an advertiser’s artwork arrives with a brand guideline instead.

Two surfaces with an objection attached rather than a veto

Sponsored search result

Recorded rather than decided, and it is what has to be answered before this flag flips. /search’s whole subject is the extent of an index and the order of its results. A paid row is either ordered among the organic ones, which is buying an ordering, or it is not a result at all — in which case it is a band on a page a reader came to in order to leave quickly.

search

Sponsored map result

Recorded rather than decided. A mark on a map here is drawn only where the page can name it — 176 of 176 across the fifty country plates — and a paid pin is a dot whose reason for existing the page cannot state. Twenty countries draw between 3.6 and 12 pixels wide on the instrument, so 'visually distinguishable' is a measurement somebody has to make before this can ship.
