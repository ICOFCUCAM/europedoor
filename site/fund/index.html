Europe Fund

What travel leaves behind.

Tourism arrives in a place and takes something out of it — a path, a language, a harbour wall, a summer. The Fund is the mechanism for putting something back, listed publicly, project by project, with the local partner named.

The Fund holds no money, and will not until three things are true

There is an operating entity; a regulated payment path with a named payee; and a written answer on how contributions are treated in each country we would collect in. Until then this is a register of projects and nothing else — there is no balance, no total raised, and no donate button anywhere on this site. The data files are validated to reject a project that carries an amount.

12 projects on the register

Chosen for being small enough that a travel platform could plausibly matter to them, and specific enough that you could go and look at the result.

Romania

Saxon fortified churches, Transylvania

Around 150 fortified churches were built by German-speaking settlers in Transylvania between the 13th and 16th centuries. Most of the community that maintained them left after 1990, and the roofs are the first thing to go.

Heritage repair · listed

Romania

Hay meadows and the people who cut them

Transylvania's hay meadows hold some of the richest plant diversity in Europe, and they hold it because they are cut by hand at the right time each year. Mechanised cutting destroys them within a decade.

Landscape · listed

Romania

Wooden churches of Maramureș

Shingle-roofed oak churches built without nails, some from the 1600s, in villages of a few hundred people. Shingles last about thirty years and the craft of splitting them is held by a handful of people.

Heritage repair · listed

Norway

Sámi-language media and youth programmes

There are ten Sámi languages and several have fewer than a thousand speakers. Media and youth programming in the language does more for its survival than any monument.

Language · listed

Ireland

Dry stone walls, Inis Mór

Sixteen hundred kilometres of dry stone wall on an island fourteen kilometres long, built to clear the ground and to make soil out of sand and seaweed. They fall down and are rebuilt by hand or not at all.

Landscape · listed

Türkiye

Coast path erosion, the Lycian Way

540 kilometres of waymarked path opened in 1999, now carrying far more feet than it was built for, across soft limestone and through villages with no maintenance budget.

Trails · in-progress

North Macedonia

Lake Ohrid endemic species monitoring

Between three and five million years old, with over sixty species found nowhere else, and under pressure from sewage, water abstraction and shoreline building.

Landscape · listed

Albania

Guesthouse training in the Albanian Alps

The Valbona-Theth crossing has gone from almost no visitors to thousands a season within a decade, and the money mostly stays local because the beds are family-run. That is fragile and worth protecting.

Community · in-progress

Greece

Sephardic Thessaloniki, documented

Thessaloniki was a majority-Sephardic city for four centuries. Almost the entire community was deported in 1943. The documentation of what was there is held in fragments across several countries.

Memory · listed

Georgia

Qvevri makers, Kakheti

The clay vessels Georgian wine ferments in are made by a handful of families. The technique is on the UNESCO intangible heritage list and the number of working makers is in single figures.

Craft · listed

Netherlands

Wadden Sea mudflat guiding

Walking the exposed sea floor at low tide requires a guide who knows the channels and the tide tables, and the licensing and training of those guides is entirely voluntary work.

Trails · listed

Romania

Bear coexistence in the Carpathians

Romania holds most of Europe's brown bears. Conflict with people rises every year, driven largely by roadside feeding and unsecured rubbish, and the animals lose that argument.
