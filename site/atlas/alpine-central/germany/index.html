Alpine & Central Europe

Germany

Sixteen states that were separate countries within living memory, and still behave like it.

Germany has no single centre because it was assembled from kingdoms and free cities in 1871, and every one of them kept its own capital, cathedral and beer. That is the country's real pleasure: Bavaria and Hamburg have less in common than France and Spain. Berlin is a city of the twentieth century, still visibly working through it; the Rhine, the Black Forest and the Baltic coast are all quieter than they deserve.

Getting around

ICE trains link the big cities fast, and the Deutschlandticket gives a month of unlimited regional transport nationwide for a flat fee — the best transport deal in Europe. Regional trains reach almost every valley.

When to come

May and September are the best of it. December is Christmas market season and genuinely worth the cold — the markets are civic events, not tourist ones. Oktoberfest is in September, which catches people out annually.

Worth knowing

  • Cash is still king in a surprising number of bakeries, bars and taxis.
  • Sunday closing is near-total; buy food on Saturday.
  • Jaywalking against a red man is genuinely disapproved of, especially with children present.

At the table

  • Bread: 3,000 registered varieties, and the bakery is the first thing to find
  • Wurst, which is regional and non-negotiable — Currywurst in Berlin, Weisswurst before noon only in Munich
  • Riesling from the Mosel, which is dry now whatever your grandmother thought
