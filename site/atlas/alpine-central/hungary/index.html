Alpine & Central Europe

Hungary

A thermal-water capital on the Danube, a language related to almost nothing, and sweet wine that outlived empires.

Hungary sits on a plain of hot springs — Budapest alone has over a hundred, and the bathing culture runs from Roman to Ottoman to imperial. The language is Finno-Ugric and unrelated to any neighbour, which has shaped a certain separateness. Beyond the capital there is Lake Balaton, the volcanic wine hills above it, and Tokaj, where botrytised wine was made a century before Sauternes.

Getting around

Budapest's metro line 1 is the oldest on the continent after London and still running. Trains reach Balaton, Eger and Tokaj slowly and cheaply. The capital is walkable and its trams are useful.

When to come

Budapest is good year-round and the outdoor thermal baths are best in January, steaming in the cold. Balaton is a summer place and dead outside it. Harvest in Tokaj is October, late.

Worth knowing

  • The forint, not the euro; exchange rates at street bureaux are usually terrible.
  • In the baths, bring flip-flops and a towel or hire both; some pools require a cap.
  • Ruin bars occupy derelict courtyards in the old Jewish quarter, which is also a memorial district — the two coexist awkwardly.

At the table

  • Paprika, in everything, and not interchangeable with chilli
  • Goulash, which is a soup here and not a stew
  • Tokaji Aszú, sweet, ancient and unlike any other dessert wine
