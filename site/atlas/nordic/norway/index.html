The Nordics

Norway

A country shaped by water that had somewhere to be.

Norway is long — its coastline, unfolded, would wrap a good way round the world — and almost everything worth seeing sits where rock meets salt water. The interior is high plateau and slow trains; the coast is fjord, ferry and fishing town. Prices are the highest in Europe and the light is the strangest: midnight sun above the Arctic Circle in June, four-hour days in December.

Getting around

The Bergen and Dovre railways are two of Europe's great train rides and cost less than the equivalent flight if booked early. Coastal Norway runs on ferries and express boats — treat them as public transport, not excursions. North of Trondheim, distances get serious: fly, or take the coastal ferry and give it days rather than hours.

When to come

June and July give you the midnight sun and open mountain roads. Late September brings colour and empty trails. Late January to March is the northern lights and ski season; November is neither.

Worth knowing

  • Card and phone payment work everywhere, including on ferries and in mountain huts. Cash is close to useless.
  • Allemannsretten — the right to roam — lets you walk and camp on uncultivated land, with obligations attached: no fires in summer, no trace, 150 metres from any house.
  • Alcohol above 4.7% is sold only in state Vinmonopolet shops, which close early and shut on Sundays.

At the table

  • Brown cheese (brunost) — caramelised whey, sliced thin, an acquired and worthwhile taste
  • Cured and smoked salmon, and the fresher, less-famous cod
  • Kanelbolle: the cinnamon bun that fuels every hike
