The Adriatic & Balkans

Bosnia and Herzegovina

Where the Ottoman and Austro-Hungarian worlds meet in one street, and the 1990s are still within touching distance.

Bosnia is mountainous, green and river-cut, with a capital where a mosque, a synagogue, an Orthodox and a Catholic church stand within a few hundred metres. The war of 1992–95 is visible in the buildings and unavoidable in conversation, and Bosnians discuss it more openly than visitors expect. It is one of the cheapest and most hospitable countries in Europe.

Getting around

Buses are the network and they are frequent and cheap. The Sarajevo–Mostar railway is one of the most scenic short rides in Europe. A car reaches the mountain villages and the river canyons.

When to come

Spring and early autumn are ideal. Sarajevo is hot and hazy in midsummer and snowy in winter — it hosted the 1984 Winter Olympics and still skis. Mostar in August is very hot and very full.

Worth knowing

  • Landmines still exist in some rural areas; stay on marked paths and heed the signs.
  • The convertible mark is pegged to the euro and euros are often accepted in tourist areas.
  • The country has two entities and three presidencies; flags and signage change meaningfully between towns.

At the table

  • Ćevapi: small grilled minced-meat rolls in somun bread with raw onion
  • Burek from a pekara, sold by weight, and never containing anything but meat — the others have other names
  • Bosnian coffee, served with rahat lokum and a copper set, and drunk slowly
