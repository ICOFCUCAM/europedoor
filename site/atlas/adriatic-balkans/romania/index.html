The Adriatic & Balkans

Romania

Saxon fortified churches, painted monasteries, and the largest population of brown bears in Europe.

Romania is a Latin language surrounded by Slavic ones, with a German-founded Transylvania in the middle and an Orthodox monastic north. It has Europe's largest remaining virgin forest, some 6,000 brown bears, and hay meadows farmed by scythe in villages where that never stopped. Bucharest carries the marks of Ceaușescu's demolitions and the Belle Époque city he demolished.

Getting around

Trains are slow but reach the Transylvanian towns and the Danube. Buses and minibuses fill the gaps. A car is close to essential for the Saxon villages and the painted monasteries.

When to come

May and June for hay meadows and wildflowers, September for the light and the food. The Transfăgărășan road opens roughly July to October and is closed by snow the rest of the year. Winter is cold and the Carpathian ski areas are cheap.

Worth knowing

  • Bears are genuinely present in the Carpathians; do not feed them from a car, which is both illegal and how people get hurt.
  • The leu, not the euro.
  • Village guesthouses (pensiuni) are the best accommodation in the country and cost very little.

At the table

  • Sarmale: cabbage rolls with polenta and sour cream
  • Ciorbă, a sour soup, in dozens of versions
  • Țuică and pălincă, plum spirits, home-distilled and strong
