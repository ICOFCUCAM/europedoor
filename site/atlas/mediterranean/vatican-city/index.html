Vatican City

The smallest state in the world, at 0.49 square kilometres, and one of its largest art collections.

Vatican City is an independent state inside Rome, created by treaty in 1929, with around 800 residents and no tourism industry of its own — visitors come for the basilica and the museums. It holds the Sistine Chapel, Raphael's rooms, and a collection assembled over five centuries. It is entered freely on foot from St Peter's Square; there is no border post.

Getting around

On foot. Ottaviano and Cipro on Rome's metro line A are the nearest stations; the museum entrance and the basilica entrance are on opposite sides of the walls, a fifteen-minute walk apart.

When to come

The museums are least crowded on a Tuesday or Thursday afternoon and worst on Mondays and rainy days. Papal audiences on Wednesday mornings fill St Peter's Square and make the basilica difficult.

Worth knowing

  • Covered shoulders and knees are required and enforced at the door, in any weather.
  • The basilica is free; the museums are ticketed and should be booked online.
  • Photography is banned inside the Sistine Chapel, which is stated repeatedly and ignored constantly.
