The Caucasus & the Bosphorus

Azerbaijan

Fire that comes out of the ground, a walled medieval city, and oil money spent on architecture.

Azerbaijan sits on the Caspian, on gas reserves that have been burning at the surface for millennia — the Zoroastrian fire temples were built around them. Baku pairs a walled old city with a skyline of flame-shaped towers. Beyond the capital the Greater Caucasus rises to 4,000 metres with mountain villages that kept their own languages, and mud volcanoes cover the semi-desert south.

Getting around

A modern railway links Baku to Ganja and Sheki. Buses and shared taxis cover the rest. Mountain villages like Xinaliq need a 4x4. The Armenian border is closed.

When to come

Spring and autumn; Baku is hot and windy in summer and the mountains are best from June to September. Novruz in March is the biggest holiday of the year.

Worth knowing

  • The border with Armenia is closed; an Armenian stamp can complicate entry.
  • Baku's old city and the Gobustan petroglyphs are both UNESCO-listed.
  • Tea houses are a social institution and usually male-dominated outside the capital.

At the table

  • Plov, in dozens of versions, cooked in a pastry crust for feasts
  • Dolma and kebab, argued over with every neighbour
  • Tea from a pear-shaped armudu glass, with jam rather than sugar
