Monaco

Two square kilometres of rock, casino and Grand Prix circuit, wedged between France and the sea.

Monaco is the second-smallest country in the world and the most densely populated, built vertically up a cliff because there is nowhere else to go. It is not in the EU or Schengen in its own right, though you enter through France and will not notice a border. For a visitor it is a half-day: the old town on the rock, the casino, the harbour, and the fact that a Formula One circuit runs through all three.

Getting around

Walk, and use the public lifts — a network of them climbs the cliff for free. The train from Nice takes twenty minutes and is by far the best way in.

When to come

The Grand Prix takes the last weekend in May and doubles every price in the region. Spring and autumn are warm and manageable; August is hot and full.

Worth knowing

  • Not in the EU or Schengen as a member, but inside the Schengen travel area in practice via France.
  • The casino requires ID and refuses entry to Monégasque citizens by law.
  • Beach access is largely paid; the free public beach is at Larvotto.

At the table

  • Barbagiuan, a fried chard and ricotta pastry, the actual national dish
  • Socca and Niçoise cooking, shared with the coast either side
  • A coffee at the Café de Paris, priced as theatre
