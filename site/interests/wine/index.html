Europe/Experiences/Wine & drink

Travelling for

Wine & drink

41 cities across 18 countries are tagged for this. The Journey Planner weights the same tag, so what you see here is what it will build from.

Austria · Vienna & the East

Dürnstein & the Wachau

A blue baroque tower over the Danube, terraced vineyards behind, and the castle where Richard the Lionheart was held.

Croatia · Istria & Kvarner

Motovun

A walled hill town over a truffle forest, with a walkable rampart and a view of the whole Mirna valley.

Croatia · Dalmatia

Vis

The furthest inhabited Dalmatian island, closed to foreigners as a military base until 1989 and undeveloped because of it.

Cyprus · The Troodos Mountains

Omodos & the wine villages

Cobbled mountain villages growing indigenous grapes nobody else has, around a 12th-century monastery.

Czechia · Moravia

Mikulov

A chateau town on the Austrian border, surrounded by the country's best vineyards and a limestone ridge.

France · The Loire & the Southwest

Amboise & the Loire châteaux

A royal town on the Loire, with Leonardo's last house at the top of it and Chenonceau downstream.

France · Provence & the Mediterranean

Avignon

Seven popes ruled from here in the 14th century and left the largest gothic palace in the world behind them.

France · The Alps & the East

Beaune

The wine capital of Burgundy, inside its own walls, on the most valuable agricultural land on earth.

France · The Loire & the Southwest

Biarritz & the Basque Coast

Atlantic surf, a Napoleonic resort, and a food culture that belongs to a nation without a state.

France · The Loire & the Southwest

Bordeaux

An 18th-century stone city on a river bend, cleaned in the 2000s, with the world's most famous vineyards at the gates.

France · Provence & the Mediterranean

Carcassonne

A double-walled citadel of 52 towers, heavily restored in the 1850s and argued about ever since.

France · The Alps & the East

Colmar & the Alsace wine road

Half-timbered, geranium-hung, and German four times in a century — the food and the wine still say so.

France · Paris & Île-de-France

Paris

Nineteenth-century town planning at monumental scale, with the world's densest concentration of art inside it.

France · Champagne & the North

Reims

Where thirty-three French kings were crowned, above twenty kilometres of Roman chalk cellars full of champagne.

France · The Alps & the East

Strasbourg

A cathedral of pink sandstone over a half-timbered island, and the European Parliament twenty minutes away.

Georgia · Tbilisi & Kakheti

Sighnaghi & Kakheti

A walled hill town over the Alazani valley, with the Caucasus on the horizon and vineyards to the foot of them.

Georgia · Tbilisi & Kakheti

Tbilisi

Wooden balconies, a fortress above, sulphur baths below, and Persian, Russian and Soviet layers in the same street.

Germany · The Rhine & the West

Bacharach & the Rhine Gorge

Half-timbered wine village under a ruined chapel, in the 65-kilometre UNESCO stretch of castles.

Greece · The Islands

Kefalonia

The largest Ionian island: an underground lake, a mountain of black fir, and the loggerhead turtles offshore.

Greece · The Islands

Santorini

The rim of a drowned volcano, with villages on the edge of a 300-metre cliff and vines grown in baskets on the ground.

Hungary · Balaton & the Wine Country

Tihany, Lake Balaton

A lavender peninsula with an abbey on a cliff, sticking into a shallow warm lake.

Hungary · Balaton & the Wine Country

Tokaj

The world's first classified wine region, from 1737, on volcanic slopes at a river confluence.

Italy · Tuscany & the Centre

Florence

A banking city that funded the Renaissance and kept nearly all of it inside a twenty-minute walk.

Italy · Rome & Lazio

Orvieto

A town on a plug of volcanic rock with a striped cathedral and an Etruscan city tunnelled underneath it.

Italy · The North & the Dolomites

Turin

The first capital of Italy: baroque arcades, an Egyptian museum second only to Cairo's, and vermouth invented in a bar here.

Italy · The North & the Dolomites

Vernazza & the Cinque Terre

Five fishing villages on a cliff coast, joined by terraced footpaths and a railway in a tunnel.

Italy · The North & the Dolomites

Verona

A Roman arena still used for opera, a river bend of pink marble streets, and Valpolicella in the hills above.

Liechtenstein · The Rhine Valley & the Mountains

Vaduz

A capital of 5,700 people under a castle the prince actually lives in, with a black concrete art museum on the main street.

Luxembourg · The Capital & the Moselle

Remich & the Moselle

Terraced vineyards on a slow river, with Germany on the far bank and Crémant cellars on this one.

Moldova · Chișinău & the Cellars

Chișinău

A green, low-rise capital of parks and Soviet-era planning, with the wine country immediately outside it.

Portugal · Porto & the Douro

Pinhão & the Douro valley

Terraced schist vineyards falling into a dammed river, worked by hand because machines cannot stand up on them.

Portugal · Porto & the Douro

Porto

Steep, granite and blue-tiled, with port lodges across the river and an ocean at the end of the tram line.

Portugal · The Alentejo & the South

Évora

A walled town with a Roman temple in the middle, a chapel of bones, and megaliths in the fields around it.

Serbia · Belgrade & Vojvodina

Novi Sad

A Habsburg river city under the Petrovaradin fortress, with wine hills behind it.

Slovakia · Bratislava & the Southwest

Bratislava

A compact old town under a white castle, with a communist-era bridge and a UFO on top of it.

Spain · Catalonia & the East

Girona & the Empordà

A walled river city with the widest gothic nave in the world, forty minutes from Barcelona and a fraction of the crowd.

Spain · Green Spain

Ribeira Sacra

Vineyards on canyon walls above the Sil, worked from boats and by hand, with Romanesque monasteries in the woods.

Spain · Andalusia

Ronda

A town split by a 120-metre gorge with an 18th-century bridge across it, in the middle of white-village country.

Spain · Catalonia & the East

Tarragona

The Roman capital of Hispania, with an amphitheatre on the beach and an aqueduct in the woods.

United Kingdom · Scotland

Islay

A low green island of nine working distilleries, 3,000 people and 50,000 wintering geese.

United Kingdom · Northern Ireland

The Causeway Coast (Bushmills)

Forty thousand basalt columns, a rope bridge to a fishing rock, and cliffs the whole way along.
