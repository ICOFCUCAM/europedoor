Europe/Experiences/Architecture

Travelling for

Architecture

126 cities across 43 countries are tagged for this. The Journey Planner weights the same tag, so what you see here is what it will build from.

Albania · Tirana, the Riviera & the South

Gjirokastër

A UNESCO-listed Ottoman town of stone-roofed tower houses on a steep hillside under a castle.

Austria · Tyrol & the West

Innsbruck

A university city at 574 metres with a 2,300-metre mountain reachable from the centre in twenty minutes.

Austria · Salzburg & the Salzkammergut

Salzburg

Baroque built on salt money, under a fortress, with Mozart born in the middle of it.

Austria · Vienna & the East

Vienna

Habsburg on the outside, Freud and Klimt and social housing on the inside, with the world's best-funded music scene.

Azerbaijan · Baku & the Caspian

Baku

A UNESCO-listed walled city with a mysterious stone tower, ringed by oil-boom mansions and three flame-shaped skyscrapers.

Azerbaijan · The Northwest & the Mountains

Sheki

A silk road town under wooded mountains, with a khan's summer palace whose windows are built without glue or nails.

Belarus · Minsk & the Centre

Minsk

A city rebuilt from almost nothing after 1944 along one monumental avenue, and unusually complete as a piece of urban design.

Belgium · Flanders

Bruges

A trading city frozen when its river silted up in the 1500s, which is why it looks like this.

Belgium · Brussels & Wallonia

Brussels

Art Nouveau streets, comic-strip walls, a gilded square, and the machinery of a continent in the eastern quarter.

Bosnia and Herzegovina · Herzegovina

Mostar

A 16th-century Ottoman bridge, destroyed in 1993 and rebuilt from the river stones, over a green-blue river.

Bulgaria · Thrace & the Black Sea

Plovdiv

One of Europe's oldest continuously inhabited cities, on six hills, with a Roman theatre still in use.

Croatia · Dalmatia

Dubrovnik

A maritime republic in near-perfect limestone, walled, car-free, and under real pressure from its own popularity.

Croatia · Istria & Kvarner

Rovinj

A Venetian fishing town on a former island, with a campanile copied from San Marco and houses straight into the sea.

Croatia · Inland Croatia

Zagreb

A central European capital of coffee terraces and museums, with almost nothing Mediterranean about it.

Czechia · Moravia

Brno

Czechia's second city: student, modernist and self-deprecating, with an ossuary of 50,000 under the square.

Czechia · Prague & Bohemia

Prague

Romanesque, gothic, baroque and cubist stacked on top of each other, with a castle above and a bridge in between.

Czechia · Prague & Bohemia

Český Krumlov

A castle town in a tight loop of the Vltava, essentially unchanged since the 18th century.

Denmark · Zealand & Copenhagen

Copenhagen

A harbour city clean enough to swim in the middle of, with the densest good eating in the Nordics.

Estonia · Tallinn & the North Coast

Tallinn

A walled hanseatic town where the merchants' houses, the guild halls and the city wall all survived, and a startup district grew up beside them.

Finland · Helsinki & the Southern Coast

Helsinki

Neoclassical centre, brutal-modern edges, and a design district you can walk in an afternoon.

France · The Loire & the Southwest

Amboise & the Loire châteaux

A royal town on the Loire, with Leonardo's last house at the top of it and Chenonceau downstream.

France · Champagne & the North

Amiens

The largest gothic cathedral in France by volume, and market gardens on canals behind it.

France · Provence & the Mediterranean

Avignon

Seven popes ruled from here in the 14th century and left the largest gothic palace in the world behind them.

France · The Loire & the Southwest

Bordeaux

An 18th-century stone city on a river bend, cleaned in the 2000s, with the world's most famous vineyards at the gates.

France · Provence & the Mediterranean

Carcassonne

A double-walled citadel of 52 towers, heavily restored in the 1850s and argued about ever since.

France · Paris & Île-de-France

Chartres

The most complete Gothic cathedral in France, with 172 windows of 12th-century glass that survived two wars in boxes.

France · The Auvergne & the Massif Central

Clermont-Ferrand

A city built in black volcanic stone under a chain of eighty dormant volcanoes.

France · The Alps & the East

Colmar & the Alsace wine road

Half-timbered, geranium-hung, and German four times in a century — the food and the wine still say so.

France · Champagne & the North

Lille

Flemish in its architecture and French in its administration, with the best food market north of Lyon.

France · Normandy & Brittany

Mont-Saint-Michel

An abbey on a rock in a bay with the fastest tides in Europe, and a village winding up to it.

France · Paris & Île-de-France

Paris

Nineteenth-century town planning at monumental scale, with the world's densest concentration of art inside it.

France · Champagne & the North

Reims

Where thirty-three French kings were crowned, above twenty kilometres of Roman chalk cellars full of champagne.

France · The Loire & the Southwest

Sarlat & the Dordogne

A honey-coloured medieval town in a valley of castles, walnut groves and the oldest painted caves in Europe.

France · The Alps & the East

Strasbourg

A cathedral of pink sandstone over a half-timbered island, and the European Parliament twenty minutes away.

France · The Loire & the Southwest

Toulouse

The pink city: brick everything, a Romanesque basilica, aerospace money and a canal to the Mediterranean.

Georgia · Tbilisi & Kakheti

Tbilisi

Wooden balconies, a fortress above, sulphur baths below, and Persian, Russian and Soviet layers in the same street.

Germany · Berlin & the East

Berlin

Poor, sexy, enormous and unfinished — five museums on one island and a wall memorial that refuses to be comfortable.

Germany · Berlin & the East

Dresden

A baroque royal capital burned in 1945 and rebuilt stone by numbered stone, on a river bend.

Germany · The North

Hamburg

A merchant republic and Europe's third port, with more bridges than Venice and Amsterdam together.

Germany · The North

Lübeck

The queen of the Hanseatic League, on an island, in brick gothic, and the home of marzipan.

Greece · The Islands

Corfu

Venetian for four centuries and British for fifty, green all year, with an old town that looks nothing like the Aegean.

Greece · The Islands

Heraklion & Knossos

Crete's capital and the Minoan palace beside it — Europe's oldest city, and a reconstruction people still argue about.

Greece · Athens & the Peloponnese

Mystras

A Byzantine city on a spur of Taygetos, abandoned in the 1950s and left standing: palaces, churches and frescoes.

Greece · The Islands

Rhodes

A walled medieval city built by crusader knights, on an island of Ottoman villages and butterfly valleys.

Greece · The Islands

Symi

Neoclassical mansions in ochre and blue stacked round a horseshoe harbour, an hour from Rhodes.

Hungary · Budapest & the Danube Bend

Budapest

Hilly Buda and flat Pest, joined by bridges, with a parliament on the water and a hundred hot springs underneath.

Italy · Tuscany & the Centre

Arezzo

A hill town with Piero della Francesca's greatest fresco cycle and none of Florence's queue.

Italy · Umbria & the Marches

Assisi

A pink stone town on Monte Subasio, built around the basilica of a man who rejected everything it is made of.

Italy · The North & the Dolomites

Bellagio & Lake Como

A village on the point where the lake divides, with villa gardens on both arms and ferries between them.

Italy · Tuscany & the Centre

Bologna

Forty kilometres of porticoes, the oldest university in the world, and the best eating in Italy.

Italy · Rome & Lazio

Civita di Bagnoregio

A village on an eroding tufa pillar, reached by a footbridge, with about a dozen permanent residents left.

Italy · Tuscany & the Centre

Florence

A banking city that funded the Renaissance and kept nearly all of it inside a twenty-minute walk.

Italy · The South & the Islands

Lecce

Baroque carved in soft golden limestone, in the heel of Italy, an hour from two seas.

Italy · Tuscany & the Centre

Lucca

An intact Renaissance wall, four kilometres round, wide enough on top to be a park, with a Roman amphitheatre turned into a square inside it.

Italy · The South & the Islands

Matera

Cave dwellings inhabited for nine thousand years, evacuated in 1952 as a national disgrace, now a UNESCO site.

Italy · Rome & Lazio

Orvieto

A town on a plug of volcanic rock with a striped cathedral and an Etruscan city tunnelled underneath it.

Italy · The South & the Islands

Palermo

Norman, Arab and Byzantine in the same building, with the best street food in Italy underneath.

Italy · Tuscany & the Centre

Siena

A medieval city that lost to Florence and was preserved by losing, arranged around a shell-shaped square.

Italy · The North & the Dolomites

Trieste

Austro-Hungarian, Italian, Slovene and Jewish at once, on a limestone edge above the Adriatic, with the best coffee culture in Italy.

Italy · The North & the Dolomites

Turin

The first capital of Italy: baroque arcades, an Egyptian museum second only to Cairo's, and vermouth invented in a bar here.
