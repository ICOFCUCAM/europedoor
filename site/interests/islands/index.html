Travelling for

Islands

26 of the 319 destinations in this atlas are tagged islands. One of the narrowest tags in this atlas, which makes it a real filter — and a short list is a gap in the writing as much as a fact about Europe.

8% of the atlas · 14 of 50 countries · the 12th widest of 17 tags

Croatia · Dalmatia

Vis

The furthest inhabited Dalmatian island, closed to foreigners as a military base until 1989 and undeveloped because of it.

Estonia · The Islands & the South

Kuressaare, Saaremaa

An island town around a moated bishop's castle, on Estonia's largest and slowest island.

Finland · Helsinki & the Southern Coast

Helsinki

Neoclassical centre, brutal-modern edges, and a design district you can walk in an afternoon.

France · Corsica

Bonifacio

A citadel on white limestone cliffs undercut by the sea, with Sardinia visible across twelve kilometres of strait.

France · The Loire & the Southwest

La Rochelle

A Protestant merchant port behind three medieval towers, with an island of white houses across a bridge.

Greece · The Islands

Corfu

Venetian for four centuries and British for fifty, green all year, with an old town that looks nothing like the Aegean.

Greece · Athens & the Peloponnese

Hydra

An island two hours from Athens with no cars and no bicycles at all — mules, boats and feet.

Greece · The Islands

Ikaria

A steep island where people routinely live past ninety, festivals start at midnight and shops open when they open.

Greece · The Islands

Kefalonia

The largest Ionian island: an underground lake, a mountain of black fir, and the loggerhead turtles offshore.

Greece · The Islands

Milos

A volcanic island of white rock formations, coloured mining beaches and boat-only coves.

Greece · The Islands

Naxos

The largest Cycladic island: mountain villages, marble quarries, a working agricultural interior and long sandy beaches.

Greece · The Islands

Paros

Cycladic in the way people imagine the Cyclades, with marble quarries that supplied the ancient world and a working town behind the beaches.

Greece · The Islands

Santorini

The rim of a drowned volcano, with villages on the edge of a 300-metre cliff and vines grown in baskets on the ground.

Greece · The Islands

Symi

Neoclassical mansions in ochre and blue stacked round a horseshoe harbour, an hour from Rhodes.

Ireland · The West & the Atlantic

The Aran Islands (Inis Mór)

Three limestone islands where Irish is the daily language and stone walls divide fields the size of rooms.

Italy · The South & the Islands

Alghero, Sardinia

A Catalan-speaking walled port on Sardinia's northwest coast, with sea caves and Bronze Age towers inland.

Italy · The South & the Islands

Lipari & the Aeolians

Seven volcanic islands off Sicily, one of them erupting every twenty minutes, all of them on the UNESCO list.

Lithuania · The Coast & Centre

Nida, Curonian Spit

Wooden fishermen's houses under a moving dune, on a sand spit between lagoon and sea.

Malta · Gozo & Comino

Victoria, Gozo

A citadel town in the middle of an island where the pace drops noticeably the moment the ferry docks.

Netherlands · The North & the Wadden Islands

Texel

The largest Wadden island: dunes, sheep, seals and thirty kilometres of empty North Sea beach.

Norway · Northern Norway

Lofoten (Svolvær)

Granite peaks that rise straight from the sea, red fishermen's cabins below them, and cod drying on racks every spring.

Norway · Sørlandet & the South Coast

Risør

'The white town by the Skagerrak' — 300 wooden houses round a harbour, and a wooden boat festival each August.

Norway · Northern Norway

Senja

Norway's second-largest island — Lofoten's landscape without Lofoten's traffic, and almost no one on the trails.

Spain · The Balearic Islands

Ciutadella, Menorca

Menorca's old capital: a sandstone harbour town on an island that is a UNESCO biosphere reserve and mostly farmland.

Sweden · Stockholm & the Archipelago

Stockholm

Water on every side, a preserved medieval core, and the best-funded museums in the Nordics.

United Kingdom · Scotland

Isle of Skye (Portree)

Black basalt ridges, sea cliffs and more visitors than the single-track roads were built for.

Why this page tells you its own tag is wide

A filter that matches most of a continent is not a filter, and hiding that makes the tool look better than it is. The share is counted from the dataset on every build; the sentence about it comes from one published rule — over 40% barely narrows anything, 15–40% narrows usefully, under 15% is genuinely narrow. The Journey Planner weights this same tag, so what you see here is what it will build from.
