Travelling for

Music & nightlife

41 cities across 21 countries are tagged for this. The Journey Planner weights the same tag, so what you see here is what it will build from.

Austria · Salzburg & the Salzkammergut

Salzburg

Baroque built on salt money, under a fortress, with Mozart born in the middle of it.

Austria · Vienna & the East

Vienna

Habsburg on the outside, Freud and Klimt and social housing on the inside, with the world's best-funded music scene.

Belgium · Brussels & Wallonia

Dinant

A citadel on a cliff over the Meuse, a bulbous cathedral below, and the birthplace of the saxophone.

Belgium · Flanders

Ghent

Bruges's rival, larger, younger and still a working student city rather than a museum of itself.

Czechia · Moravia

Brno

Czechia's second city: student, modernist and self-deprecating, with an ossuary of 50,000 under the square.

Czechia · Prague & Bohemia

Prague

Romanesque, gothic, baroque and cubist stacked on top of each other, with a castle above and a bridge in between.

Denmark · Zealand & Copenhagen

Copenhagen

A harbour city clean enough to swim in the middle of, with the densest good eating in the Nordics.

Denmark · Zealand & Copenhagen

Roskilde

Viking ships raised from the fjord, thirty-nine Danish monarchs in one cathedral, and a festival in a field.

Finland · The Lakeland

Savonlinna

A 15th-century castle on an island in the middle of Saimaa, which hosts opera in its courtyard each July.

Germany · Berlin & the East

Berlin

Poor, sexy, enormous and unfinished — five museums on one island and a wall memorial that refuses to be comfortable.

Germany · Berlin & the East

Dresden

A baroque royal capital burned in 1945 and rebuilt stone by numbered stone, on a river bend.

Germany · The North

Hamburg

A merchant republic and Europe's third port, with more bridges than Venice and Amsterdam together.

Germany · Berlin & the East

Leipzig

Bach's church, a peaceful revolution that started in it, and a cotton mill full of painters.

Greece · Athens & the Peloponnese

Athens

A sprawling modern capital with a 2,500-year-old temple on a rock in the middle and a food scene that got very good, quietly.

Greece · Northern Greece

Thessaloniki

Greece's second city: Byzantine, Ottoman and Sephardic layers, and the country's most confident food.

Hungary · Budapest & the Danube Bend

Budapest

Hilly Buda and flat Pest, joined by bridges, with a parliament on the water and a hundred hot springs underneath.

Iceland · Reykjavík & the Southwest

Reykjavík

The northernmost capital of a sovereign state, small enough to cross on foot and loud on Friday nights.

Ireland · The West & the Atlantic

Dingle

A fishing town on a peninsula of beehive huts, empty beaches and the westernmost point of Europe's mainland fringe.

Ireland · Dublin & the East

Dublin

Georgian squares, a 1,200-year-old river crossing, and four Nobel laureates in literature from one small city.

Ireland · The West & the Atlantic

Galway

A small port city that plays music every night of the week and treats a festival as its natural state.

Italy · Tuscany & the Centre

Bologna

Forty kilometres of porticoes, the oldest university in the world, and the best eating in Italy.

Latvia · Kurzeme & the Coast

Liepāja

A wind-blown port with a derelict tsarist naval town at its northern end and a rock music habit.

Latvia · Riga & Around

Riga

A hanseatic old town wrapped in a boulevard ring, wrapped in the best Art Nouveau streets in Europe.

Netherlands · The Randstad

Amsterdam

A 17th-century canal plan that still works, with two of the world's great collections inside it.

Norway · Fjord Norway

Bergen

A hanseatic trading port wedged between seven mountains, wet 200 days a year and entirely unbothered about it.

Norway · Northern Norway

Tromsø

A university town at 69°N with more bars per head than anywhere else in Norway, and the aurora directly overhead.

Poland · Lesser Poland & the South

Kraków

The royal capital that survived the war, with Europe's largest medieval square and a Jewish quarter that is now a district again.

Poland · Masovia & the Centre

Warsaw

Eighty-five per cent destroyed in 1944 and rebuilt from Canaletto's paintings, with a Stalinist tower and glass towers around it.

Portugal · Lisbon & the West

Lisbon

A tiled, hilly, sea-facing capital rebuilt on a grid after the 1755 earthquake, with trams that still climb the old parts.

Russia · The Northwest

Saint Petersburg

A planned imperial capital of canals and neoclassical facades, built on piles in a delta from 1703.

Serbia · Belgrade & Vojvodina

Belgrade

A fortress above the confluence of the Sava and the Danube, with floating clubs below it and brutalist New Belgrade opposite.

Serbia · Belgrade & Vojvodina

Novi Sad

A Habsburg river city under the Petrovaradin fortress, with wine hills behind it.

Spain · Andalusia

Granada

The last Moorish city in Spain, under the Sierra Nevada, with the Alhambra above it.

Spain · Madrid & the Centre

Madrid

A high plateau capital with three of the world's great galleries in one kilometre and a nightlife that starts at midnight.

Spain · Andalusia

Seville

Orange trees, the world's largest gothic cathedral, and flamenco that belongs to specific families.

Ukraine · Kyiv & the Centre

Kyiv

A city founded in the 5th century on hills above a wide river, with a cave monastery from 1051 beneath it.

Ukraine · Western Ukraine

Lviv

An Austro-Hungarian city with an intact centre on the UNESCO list, a coffee tradition and a chocolate one.

United Kingdom · Northern Ireland

Belfast

A Victorian industrial city working through its own recent past in public, with murals as the text.

United Kingdom · Wales

Cardiff

A coal port turned capital, with a fantasy castle in the middle and a rugby stadium that empties into the streets.

United Kingdom · Scotland

Edinburgh

A medieval spine running down from a castle on a plug of volcanic rock, with a Georgian grid alongside.

United Kingdom · London & the South East

London

A city of villages that grew together, with the free national collections of a former empire in the middle of it.
