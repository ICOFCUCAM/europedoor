The case for going in October

Europe's crowding problem is not the number of visitors. It is that they all arrive in the same eleven places in the same six weeks.

By EuropeDoor editorial · published 2026-08-28

responsible travelseasonscrowding

The arithmetic is unkind. A handful of European destinations absorb a disproportionate share of the continent's visitors, and they absorb them in July and August, which is also when those places are hottest, most expensive and least like themselves. The rest of the year, and the rest of the map, has capacity.

The shoulder-season argument usually gets made on grounds of virtue. It is stronger made on grounds of self-interest. Paris in October has the same museums, better light, shorter queues and cheaper hotels. Dubrovnik in May has the walls without the cruise schedule. The Greek islands in late September have warm sea and open tavernas and no ferry scrum.

There are real trade-offs and it is dishonest to pretend otherwise. Mountain lifts close. Island businesses shut from November. Some ferry routes stop entirely. A shoulder-season trip needs checking rather than assuming, and a wet October week in Galicia is a wet October week.

The second move is spatial rather than temporal: go one town along. Girona instead of Barcelona, an hour by train. Naxos instead of Santorini, on the same ferry line. Puglia instead of Amalfi. Utrecht instead of Amsterdam, twenty-five minutes. In every case the thing you came for exists in both places and the pressure exists in only one.

The reason platforms rarely say this is structural: recommendation systems optimise for confidence, and confidence comes from volume, so the places that already have the most visitors get recommended the most. That is a feedback loop, not a judgement about where is good.

EuropeDoor's Journey Planner weights shoulder months upward rather than downward, and gives a Galician fishing town the same page as Paris. Whether that changes anybody's behaviour is unproven. It is at least a different default.

One thing we will not do is describe anywhere as undiscovered. Publishing that sentence about a place is the fastest available way to make it untrue.
