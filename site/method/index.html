Europe Experience Score

The whole formula, on one page.

Six numbers appear on every city and country page. Here is exactly how each one is produced, because a score whose method is secret is a ranking, and rankings for sale are how travel sites lose their readers.

Nature

base 34; nature +26, mountains +22, coast +16, islands +12, winter +10, wild +6; +4 per matching experience (max 3), kinds: walk, wild, water

34–97, median 64

History

base 34; history +26, sacred +16, architecture +16, art +10, cities +4; +4 per matching experience (max 3), kinds: museum, sacred

34–97, median 76

Food & table

base 34; food +30, wine +22, festivals +6, cities +6; +4 per matching experience (max 3), kinds: table, cellar

34–96, median 64

Culture

base 34; art +22, architecture +18, music +16, design +14, festivals +10, sacred +8; +4 per matching experience (max 3), kinds: museum, stage, workshop

34–97, median 52

Adventure

base 34; mountains +24, nature +18, winter +16, coast +14, islands +10, rail +6; +4 per matching experience (max 3), kinds: walk, water, ride

34–97, median 58

Family

base 34; nature +14, coast +14, wild +12, history +8, islands +8, winter +6; music & nightlife −10; +5 per experience (max 3) that passes the family rule published at /experiences/family

24–95, median 56

Authenticity

base 34 + 10; quiet +22; not the capital +8; tagged big-city −8; +6 per experience (max 3) of kind workshop, table, wild, cellar; +2 per recorded place (max 4)

36–82, median 56

Value

110 − (midpoint of the country's daily cost band − €40) × 0.382

37–97, median 85

Two dimensions this refuses to compute

The specification this came from lists ten. Eight are computable from the dataset. These two are not, and an approximation would be worse than the gap.

Accessibility

We hold no step-free access, hearing-loop or accessible-toilet data for any place in the Atlas. Deriving a number from something else would be a guess about whether a disabled traveller can get in, and that is the worst thing on this list to guess about. The gap is stated on every place page and on /accessibility.

not computed

Romance

Not measurable from anything in this dataset. Any formula would be a proxy — sunsets, coastlines, small hotels — dressed up as evidence, and the number would be doing persuasion rather than description.

not computed

Discoverability

A second, separate score, used by Discover Mode and by Beyond the Obvious. It answers one narrow question: how far is this place from being the obvious choice?

Not the capital

A capital is where a first visit goes. Everything else has to be chosen.

+22

Editorially quiet

An editor who knows the region marked it as somewhere that stays quiet in season. This is the one judgement in the score, and it is a person's.

+24

Few curated routes pass through

Our own journeys are a proxy for the obvious circuit. A place none of them reaches is off it.

+18

Not tagged for the famous things

Cities, art and architecture are what a continent is famous for. Nature, wild and sacred are what people find later.

+20

In a country we have written thinly

Where the Atlas itself is thin, the place is very likely thin in every other guide too — which is either a gap or an opportunity, and the content report says which.

+16

What discoverability is not

It is not a crowd measurement. We hold no visitor numbers, no search volume and no occupancy data for anywhere in Europe — every product that sells those is licensed, and inventing a proxy for one and calling it evidence is the thing this project exists not to do.

So the score measures obscurity within this Atlas: how far a place is from the obvious circuit as our own dataset describes it. That is a smaller claim than "undiscovered" and it is one we can actually defend. It is also not a quality score — a high number means fewer people will have told you about a place, not that it is better. 109 of 319 places score 70 or above.

How the map is drawn

Open geographic data, hosted by us, with the licence written down before the data was downloaded. No map account, no key, no third-party tile server.

Where the land comes from

Natural Earth, at 1:110 million and 1:50 million. Public domain: the licence says in as many words that no permission is needed and no credit is required. We credit it anyway, because a reader looking at a border is entitled to know which dataset drew it.

public domain

How it gets here

A script fetches it and records the SHA-256 of the exact bytes; a second clips it to Europe, simplifies it to three levels of detail and writes the result into the repository. Both are re-runnable, and the build fails if what is committed is not what the pipeline produces.

reproducible

What it is not

Natural Earth is a cartographic source, built to look right at a stated scale. It is not a legal or authoritative statement of where a border runs, and at the scale of a continent a border is a line several kilometres wide. Do not read a disputed frontier off this map.

cartographic

Two countries have no shape

Monaco is 2 km² and Vatican City is 0.44 km². At 1:50 million neither has a polygon at all, so both are drawn as a ringed point. Inventing an outline would have been easy, and would have been a lie about a measurement.

2 of 50

Regions are groupings, not boundaries

We hold which region a destination belongs to. We do not hold region geometry — the dataset that has it carries conditions nobody here has accepted — so a region is drawn as its own destinations with its name at the middle of them, and never as a line.

no geometry

What the scores are not

  • Not measurements. They are computed from our own editorial tags. If we tag a city wrongly, its score is wrong, and the fix is to fix the tag — tell us.
  • Not quality. A 94 for Adventure means the place is about adventure, not that it does it better than a 71.
  • Not for sale. No business, tourism board or partner can change a score. There is no mechanism to; the numbers are recomputed from the dataset on every build.
  • Not capped at 100. The ceiling is 97 and the floor is 12, so nothing is ever perfect and nothing is ever worthless.

Why value is different

Five dimensions are structural — they come from tags. Value is the only one anchored to something outside our own judgement: the midpoint of the country's daily cost band, which is published on every country page and can be argued with directly.

Recomputed, never stored

No score is written into the data files. Every number on the site is derived at build time from the tags, so a score and its explanation cannot drift apart.
