At their best in October

Peak season: the weather works, everything is open, and so is everyone else's calendar.

Armenia

The first state to adopt Christianity, in 301, with monasteries in gorges and a mountain it can see but not reach.

€25–70 a day

Azerbaijan

Fire that comes out of the ground, a walled medieval city, and oil money spent on architecture.

€35–95 a day

Cyprus

The last divided capital in Europe, on an island of Byzantine churches and mountain wine.

€55–135 a day

Georgia

Eight thousand years of winemaking, its own alphabet, and mountains higher than the Alps.

€25–75 a day

Italy

A country invented in 1861 out of places that still think of themselves first.

€70–175 a day

Malta

Three inhabited islands of limestone, holding temples older than the pyramids and a city built by knights.

€60–145 a day

Moldova

The world's largest wine cellar, measured in kilometres, under one of Europe's least visited countries.

€25–60 a day

Portugal

Europe's western edge, facing an ocean it once mapped, with the oldest fixed borders on the continent.

€50–125 a day

Spain

Several countries wearing one flag, with the second-highest average altitude in Europe.

€60–150 a day

Türkiye

A city on two continents, and a Mediterranean coast with more classical ruins than Greece.

€30–95 a day

Vatican City

The smallest state in the world, at 0.49 square kilometres, and one of its largest art collections.

€60–150 a day

Quieter, and often better, in October

Shoulder season. The Journey Planner scores these upward rather than downward for exactly this month.

Albania

Closed to the world until 1991, and now the least developed and least expensive coast on the Mediterranean.

€25–75 a day

Austria

An imperial capital with a small country attached, most of it vertical.

€85–190 a day

Belgium

Three languages, six governments, the best beer in the world and a genuine talent for not explaining itself.

€75–165 a day

Bosnia and Herzegovina

Where the Ottoman and Austro-Hungarian worlds meet in one street, and the 1990s are still within touching distance.

€30–80 a day

Bulgaria

Thracian gold, Orthodox monasteries, rose fields and the cheapest good skiing in Europe.

€30–80 a day

Croatia

A thousand islands, a Roman emperor's palace still lived in, and lakes that fall into each other.

€55–140 a day

Czechia

The most beautiful city in central Europe, and a country that drinks more beer per head than any other.

€45–115 a day

France

The most visited country on earth, and still mostly empty fifty kilometres from anywhere famous.

€85–210 a day

Germany

Sixteen states that were separate countries within living memory, and still behave like it.

€70–165 a day

Greece

Six thousand islands, of which two hundred and twenty-seven are inhabited, and a mainland almost nobody visits.

€55–140 a day

Hungary

A thermal-water capital on the Danube, a language related to almost nothing, and sweet wine that outlived empires.

€40–105 a day

Kosovo

Europe's youngest country by both age and population, with medieval monasteries and a mountain trail into two neighbours.

€25–65 a day

Liechtenstein

Twenty-five kilometres long, ruled from a castle on the hill, and walkable end to end in a day.

€110–230 a day

Luxembourg

A fortress city on a gorge, a wine river, and free public transport across the whole country.

€90–190 a day

Monaco

Two square kilometres of rock, casino and Grand Prix circuit, wedged between France and the sea.

€140–320 a day

Montenegro

A drowned river canyon that looks like a fjord, with black mountains straight up behind it.

€45–115 a day

Netherlands

A country built on purpose, mostly below sea level, and cycled end to end.

€85–185 a day

North Macedonia

One of the oldest lakes on earth, a bazaar that never stopped trading, and a capital that rebuilt itself in marble.

€25–70 a day

Romania

Saxon fortified churches, painted monasteries, and the largest population of brown bears in Europe.

€30–85 a day

San Marino

The world's oldest surviving republic, on a limestone crag with three towers on the ridge.

€55–130 a day

Serbia

A confluence capital with the best nightlife in the Balkans, and monasteries frescoed eight hundred years ago.

€30–85 a day

Slovakia

The smallest high mountains in Europe, more castles per head than anywhere, and hardly anyone looking.

€40–100 a day

Slovenia

Alps, karst caves, vineyards and 46 kilometres of coast, all within two hours of each other.

€55–130 a day

Switzerland

The most expensive country in Europe and the one that most reliably justifies it.

€140–300 a day

United Kingdom

Four nations, one small island group, and an argument about all of it that has run for a thousand years.

€95–220 a day

Where we would actually send you

Quiet places in countries that are in shoulder season this month — the intersection of the two things that matter.

Albania · Tirana, the Riviera & the South

Gjirokastër

A UNESCO-listed Ottoman town of stone-roofed tower houses on a steep hillside under a castle.

Albania · The Albanian Alps

Theth

A valley village of stone houses with no road in until recently, a lock-in tower, and a waterfall above it.

Austria · Vienna & the East

Dürnstein & the Wachau

A blue baroque tower over the Danube, terraced vineyards behind, and the castle where Richard the Lionheart was held.

Belgium · Brussels & Wallonia

Dinant

A citadel on a cliff over the Meuse, a bulbous cathedral below, and the birthplace of the saxophone.

Bosnia and Herzegovina · Herzegovina

Blagaj

Bosnia and Herzegovina · Sarajevo & Central Bosnia

Travnik

The Ottoman viziers' capital for 150 years, under a fortress, with a coloured mosque and a spring beneath it.
