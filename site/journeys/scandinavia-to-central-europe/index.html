01 · The route

What the journey passes through

7 stops, in order.

▦ Big cities⚑ History & ruins⌂ Architecture♫ Music & nightlife● Food

A north-south line down the middle of the continent, from a harbour you can swim in to a capital built on hot springs. It crosses the old inner-German border, the old iron curtain, and three empires' worth of architecture, without ever needing a flight or a car. The stretch from Berlin to Budapest is the densest run of great cities in Europe.

The route, in order

Every hop is a straight-line distance between two coordinates. This atlas holds no road and no rail geometry, so that number is a floor on the leg and never the leg — Chamonix to Zermatt is 69 km here and about 170 on the ground, round a mountain range. It is here for the scale of the thing, not for planning a day.

Experiences along the way

Swim in the harbour

Free, supervised in summer, and the result of thirty years of cleaning up a working port.

Copenhagen · On the water

Smørrebrød in the correct order

Herring first, then fish, then meat, then cheese, with aquavit between — a lunch that takes three hours and is meant to.

Copenhagen · Food & table

The 62 ferry, not the tour boat

A public transport ferry down the Elbe past the container terminals; same view, a fraction of the price.

Hamburg · On the water

The Wall, where it stood

Bernauer Straße preserves the death strip, the tunnels and the names — an hour that does what no film manages.

Berlin · Walk or hike

Cycle the runways at Tempelhof

Two 2,000-metre runways, open to anyone, kept deliberately empty by referendum.

Berlin · Rail, road or ride

A pub with a tapster, not a menu

The good places pour continuously and mark your tab in pencil; you say when to stop, and the pour type is a real choice.

Prague · Food & table

Vyšehrad and the river bend

The city’s first castle, a cemetery of composers, and the whole of Prague from the ramparts without a crowd.

Prague · Walk or hike

Standing room at the Staatsoper

A few euros, queued for on the night, in one of the great opera houses. Bring a scarf to tie to the rail and hold your place.

Vienna · Music & performance

A heuriger on the city edge

Wine taverns in Vienna’s own vineyards, open only when the grower has wine to sell — a pine branch over the door means open.

Vienna · Cellar & vineyard

An outdoor thermal bath in winter

38°C water, snow on the stone, and old men playing chess on floating boards. Go early; go on a weekday.

Budapest · On the water

What you will be eating

  • Denmark — Smørrebrød: open sandwiches taken seriously, with rules about order
  • Germany — Bread: 3,000 registered varieties, and the bakery is the first thing to find
  • Czechia — Roast pork, dumplings and cabbage — vepřo knedlo zelo, the national plate
  • Austria — Wiener Schnitzel, veal and beaten thin, with potato salad
  • Hungary — Paprika, in everything, and not interchangeable with chilli

What to pack

  • An umbrella you do not mind losing
  • Comfortable shoes; these are walking cities
  • One smart layer for an opera house or a concert hall
