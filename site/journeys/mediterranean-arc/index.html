Europe/Journeys/The Mediterranean Arc

Barcelona to Athens along one sea

The Mediterranean Arc

Also from the specification: the northern shore of the Mediterranean, followed east. It changes language five times and cuisine about fifteen, and the through-line is a sea that everyone on it has been trading across for three thousand years. Ferries and trains for most of it; one flight at the end unless you have a spare week.

The route

  • Days 1–3

    Barcelona · Spain

    Start where the Mediterranean meets a grid designed in 1859.

  • Days 4–5

    Nice · France

    Over the border along the coast, into the light the painters came for.

    ↳ 490 km — a long rail day, or a short flight if the days are tight from Barcelona

  • Days 6–7

    Vernazza & the Cinque Terre · Italy

    Into Liguria and the five villages on the cliff coast.

    ↳ 199 km — a comfortable train leg, half a day at most from Nice

  • Days 8–10

    Rome · Italy

    Inland briefly, because it would be absurd not to.

    ↳ 338 km — a comfortable train leg, half a day at most from Vernazza & the Cinque Terre

  • Days 11–12

    Naples · Italy

    South for the archaeological museum and the pizza that justifies the detour.

    ↳ 188 km — a comfortable train leg, half a day at most from Rome

  • Days 13–15

    Syracuse & Ortigia · Italy

    Across to Sicily: the most powerful Greek city in the world, once.

    ↳ 429 km — a long rail day, or a short flight if the days are tight from Naples

  • Days 16–17

    Corfu · Greece

    East across the Ionian, into Venetian Greece.

    ↳ 493 km — a long rail day, or a short flight if the days are tight from Syracuse & Ortigia

  • Days 18–20

    Athens · Greece

    The end of the arc, and the beginning of most of what is on it.

    ↳ 377 km — a comfortable train leg, half a day at most from Corfu

Experiences along the way

The grid, read as a plan

Cerdà's 1859 blocks were designed with gardens inside; a few have been reopened, and finding them changes how the city reads.

Barcelona · Walk or hike

The terraces, from above

The high paths behind the villages are free, empty and better; the coastal one is the queue.

Vernazza & the Cinque Terre · Walk or hike

Testaccio, where Rome eats

The market and the streets around it are where Roman offal cooking survives; go at one, order what the sign says.

Rome · Food & table

The Appian Way on a Sunday

The road is closed to traffic on Sundays: basalt paving from 312 BC, catacombs, aqueducts and countryside, inside the city.

Rome · Walk or hike

Fried pizza on the street

Older and poorer than the baked kind, folded into a wallet and eaten walking; the sign to look for is a queue of locals.

Naples · Food & table

The Acropolis Museum, third floor

The Parthenon frieze laid out at actual scale and orientation, with plaster casts where the British Museum holds the originals — the gaps are the argument.

Athens · Museum or collection

What you will be eating

  • Spain — Tapas, which is a way of eating rather than a dish — and free with a drink in Granada and León
  • France — Bread bought twice a day, because it is worth nothing by evening
  • Italy — Pasta by region — trofie in Liguria, orecchiette in Puglia, carbonara only in Rome
  • Greece — Whatever is in the pot that day — ask to see the kitchen, which is normal

What to pack

  • Water shoes
  • Sun protection from May
  • A dry bag
  • Modest dress for churches
