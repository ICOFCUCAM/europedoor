Camino & Sacred Europe

Six places people have walked to for a thousand years

15 days · 5 stops · 5 countries · 4,780 km

✝ Sacred places⚑ History & ruins⌂ Architecture◈ Art & museums

Not a religious journey unless you want it to be — a route through the buildings and landscapes that pilgrimage produced, which is most of what Europe built before it built anything else. It ends where it should, on foot, in the rain, in Galicia. Two of these stops are still working monastic communities and one is a sovereign state.

The route, in order

  1. Days 1–2 2 nights

    Canterbury United Kingdom

    The English end: a cathedral, a murder in it, and a pilgrims' road out of London.

  2. Days 3–4 2 nights

    ↳ 316 km — a comfortable train leg, half a day at most from Canterbury

    Chartres France

    Across the Channel to 172 windows of twelfth-century glass and a labyrinth in the floor.

  3. Days 5–7 3 nights

    ↳ 1127 km — fly, or give the overland crossing a day of its own from Chartres

    Rome Italy

    South to the city everything else was measured against, and the necropolis under the basilica.

  4. Days 8–10 3 nights

    ↳ 806 km — a long rail day, or a short flight if the days are tight from Rome

    Meteora (Kalambaka) Greece

    East to six working monasteries on top of sandstone pillars, linked by monks' footpaths.

  5. Days 11–14 4 nights

    ↳ 2531 km — fly, or give the overland crossing a day of its own from Meteora (Kalambaka)

    Santiago de Compostela Spain

    The long jump west, and the last hundred kilometres walked in from Sarria.

Experiences along the way

The last day of the Pilgrims' Way

Twenty miles from Chilham along the North Downs, arriving on foot the way it was meant to be done.

Canterbury · Walk or hike

Testaccio, where Rome eats

The market and the streets around it are where Roman offal cooking survives; go at one, order what the sign says.

Rome · Food & table

The Appian Way on a Sunday

The road is closed to traffic on Sundays: basalt paving from 312 BC, catacombs, aqueducts and countryside, inside the city.

Rome · Walk or hike

The old monks' paths

Stone-cut trails link the pillars through the woods; three hours on foot beats a whole day by road.

Meteora (Kalambaka) · Walk or hike

The last hundred kilometres

From Sarria, five days, and the minimum distance for a compostela. Busy, sociable, and still the real thing.

Santiago de Compostela · Walk or hike

What you will be eating

  • United Kingdom — A Sunday roast, which is a meal and an institution
  • France — Bread bought twice a day, because it is worth nothing by evening
  • Italy — Pasta by region — trofie in Liguria, orecchiette in Puglia, carbonara only in Rome
  • Greece — Whatever is in the pot that day — ask to see the kitchen, which is normal
  • Spain — Tapas, which is a way of eating rather than a dish — and free with a drink in Granada and León

What to pack

  • Boots already broken in — this is not the trip to test them
  • A credencial if you intend to walk the last stage
  • Blister tape, before you need it
  • Something to cover shoulders in every church

What this estimate covers
