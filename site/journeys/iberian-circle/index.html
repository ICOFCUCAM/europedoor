The Iberian Circle

A loop rather than a line: down the Atlantic, across the Moorish south, up the Mediterranean and back through the middle. High-speed rail makes the long legs painless and the slow legs are where the trip actually is.

The route

  • Days 1–3

    Porto · Portugal

    Begin on the Atlantic, with port lodges across the river.

  • Days 4–6

    Lisbon · Portugal

    Down the coast: tiles, fado and the Gulbenkian.

    ↳ 274 km — a comfortable train leg, half a day at most from Porto

  • Days 7–8

    Évora · Portugal

    Inland into the Alentejo, and a Roman temple in a walled town.

    ↳ 108 km — a comfortable train leg, half a day at most from Lisbon

  • Days 9–11

    Seville · Spain

    Across the border into Andalusia.

    ↳ 213 km — a comfortable train leg, half a day at most from Évora

  • Days 12–14

    Granada · Spain

    East, to the Alhambra and free tapas.

    ↳ 213 km — a comfortable train leg, half a day at most from Seville

  • Days 15–16

    Valencia · Spain

    North up the Mediterranean for paella where the rice grows.

    ↳ 379 km — a comfortable train leg, half a day at most from Granada

  • Days 17–19

    Barcelona · Spain

    The last coastal stop, and the densest three days of the route.

    ↳ 304 km — a comfortable train leg, half a day at most from Valencia

  • Days 20–22

    Madrid · Spain

    Inland to finish: the Prado, vermouth at noon, and a train home.

    ↳ 505 km — a long rail day, or a short flight if the days are tight from Barcelona

What to pack

  • Comfortable shoes
  • Sun protection and a hat from May
  • A siesta-shaped attitude to the afternoon

What this estimate covers

About €2,670 per person: 22 nights at the moderate daily band for each country on the route, plus a distance-based transport figure between stops, plus 12%. It excludes getting to the start and home from the end, and it is planning arithmetic from published bands rather than a quote. How these numbers are made →
