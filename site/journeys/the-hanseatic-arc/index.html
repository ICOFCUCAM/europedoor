The Hanseatic Arc

Between the 13th and 17th centuries a network of merchant towns ran the northern seas, and they all built with the same brick and the same confidence. This route follows them from Flanders to the Gulf of Finland. What the League left behind is a very specific pleasure: gabled warehouses, town halls larger than the towns needed, and a shared idea of what a rich city should look like.

The route

  • Days 1–2

    Bruges · Belgium

    The southern end: a city that got rich, silted up, and was preserved by the accident.

  • Days 3–5

    Amsterdam · Netherlands

    North to the canal ring that came after the League and outdid it.

    ↳ 173 km — a comfortable train leg, half a day at most from Bruges

  • Days 6–7

    Lübeck · Germany

    East to the queen of the Hanse herself, on an island, in brick gothic.

    ↳ 421 km — a long rail day, or a short flight if the days are tight from Amsterdam

  • Days 8–10

    Gdańsk · Poland

    Along the Baltic to the Polish end of the network — and to 1939 and 1980.

    ↳ 521 km — a long rail day, or a short flight if the days are tight from Lübeck

  • Days 11–12

    Riga · Latvia

    Across the sea to the largest Art Nouveau quarter in Europe, built on the same trade.

    ↳ 448 km — a long rail day, or a short flight if the days are tight from Gdańsk

  • Days 13–15

    Tallinn · Estonia

    The last stop: the wall, the towers and the guild halls, all still standing.

    ↳ 279 km — a comfortable train leg, half a day at most from Riga

Experiences along the way

Bruges before the coaches

Day-trippers arrive from ten. Between six and nine the canals belong to you and the swans.

Bruges · Walk or hike

The city the way it is used

Rent for a day, ride out to Amsterdam-Noord on the free ferry, and stop thinking of the bicycle as an activity.

Amsterdam · Rail, road or ride

The facades of Alberta iela

Ninety minutes and a raised chin: everything worth seeing here is above the first floor.

Riga · Walk or hike

Lunch in the Zeppelin hangars

Smoked fish, black bread, curd and pickles bought from four different counters and eaten standing up.

Riga · Food & table

Walk the wall, tower to tower

Two kilometres of it still stand; the sections you can climb give the roofline the postcards use.

Tallinn · Walk or hike

Bog shoes in Lahemaa

Snowshoe-like frames let you cross raised bog off the boardwalk — an hour from the capital and utterly silent.

Tallinn · Walk or hike

What you will be eating

  • Belgium — Chips, twice fried, from a frituur, with a sauce you choose badly the first time
  • Netherlands — Raw herring, held by the tail, with onion — in season from June
  • Germany — Bread: 3,000 registered varieties, and the bakery is the first thing to find
  • Poland — Pierogi, boiled or fried, in a dozen fillings and never just one
  • Latvia — Rye bread, and rye bread ice cream, which works
  • Estonia — Black bread, dense and slightly sweet, served with everything

What to pack

  • A windproof layer; the Baltic is not warm even in July
  • Comfortable shoes for brick and cobble
  • A book for the ferry legs
