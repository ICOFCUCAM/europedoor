The Carpathian Arc

The Carpathians run 1,500 kilometres through five countries and almost nobody outside them treats the range as one thing. This does. Wooden churches, painted monasteries, hay meadows cut by hand, brown bears, and prices that make western Europe look absurd.

The route

  • Days 1–3

    Kraków · Poland

    Start in the city, then go east and stop seeing anyone.

  • Days 4–5

    Zakopane & the Tatras · Poland

    Into the Tatras, Poland's only alpine range.

    ↳ 85 km — a local train or a short drive from Kraków

  • Days 6–7

    Poprad & the High Tatras · Slovakia

    Over the border into Slovakia and the same mountains under another name.

    ↳ 37 km — a local train or a short drive from Zakopane & the Tatras

  • Days 8–9

    Levoča & the Spiš · Slovakia

    Down into the Spiš: a walled town under the largest castle ruin in central Europe.

    ↳ 22 km — a local train or a short drive from Poprad & the High Tatras

  • Days 10–12

    Sighișoara · Romania

    South into Transylvania and the Saxon citadels.

    ↳ 443 km — a long rail day, or a short flight if the days are tight from Levoča & the Spiš

  • Days 13–14

    Viscri · Romania

    A single-street village with a fortified church and horses on the road.

    ↳ 29 km — a local train or a short drive from Sighișoara

  • Days 15–16

    Brașov · Romania

    Under a wooded mountain, with a bear hide bookable at dusk.

    ↳ 61 km — a local train or a short drive from Viscri

  • Days 17–19

    Gura Humorului & the painted monasteries · Romania

    North into Bucovina, to painted monasteries that have kept their colour for five centuries.

    ↳ 212 km — a comfortable train leg, half a day at most from Brașov

What to pack

  • Boots
  • A torch for unlit churches
  • Cash for village guesthouses
  • Layers — the range makes its own weather
