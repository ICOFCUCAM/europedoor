The Carpathian Arc

Mountains, monasteries and very few visitors

20 days · 8 stops · 3 countries · 889 km in a straight line

▲ Mountains✝ Sacred places♣ Wild nature❦ Wildlife watching⚑ History & ruins

The Carpathians run 1,500 kilometres through five countries and almost nobody outside them treats the range as one thing. This does. Wooden churches, painted monasteries, hay meadows cut by hand, brown bears, and prices that make western Europe look absurd.

The route, in order

Every hop is a straight-line distance between two coordinates. This atlas holds no road and no rail geometry, so that number is a floor on the leg and never the leg — Chamonix to Zermatt is 69 km here and about 170 on the ground, round a mountain range. It is here for the scale of the thing, not for planning a day.

Experiences along the way

Lunch in a milk bar

A communist-era subsidised canteen, still running, still cheap; queue, point, pay, and eat what Poland actually eats.

Kraków · Food & table

The salt cathedral at Wieliczka

A chapel 101 metres underground with chandeliers of salt crystal, carved by miners over decades in their own time.

Kraków · Museum or collection

The walk to Morskie Oko

Nine kilometres of asphalt road that everyone does, then a steep hour above it that almost nobody does — take the second part.

Zakopane & the Tatras · Walk or hike

Hut to hut in the High Tatras

Beds, soup and beer at 1,500 metres, carried up by porters on wooden frames — a practice still going and worth watching.

Poprad & the High Tatras · Walk or hike

Haymaking with a scythe

The meadows are cut by hand because machines would destroy them; in June the villages will show you how and it is harder than it looks.

Viscri · Workshop or making

A bear hide in the Carpathians

Licensed hides run by biologists at dusk; Romania holds most of Europe's brown bears and this is the ethical way to see one.

Brașov · Wildlife & nature

The blue at Voroneț

A 1547 exterior fresco whose blue has not faded in five hundred winters, and whose recipe is still not known.

Gura Humorului & the painted monasteries · Sacred site

What you will be eating

  • Poland — Pierogi, boiled or fried, in a dozen fillings and never just one
  • Slovakia — Bryndzové halušky: potato dumplings with sheep cheese and bacon
  • Romania — Sarmale: cabbage rolls with polenta and sour cream

What to pack

  • Boots
  • A torch for unlit churches
  • Cash for village guesthouses
  • Layers — the range makes its own weather
