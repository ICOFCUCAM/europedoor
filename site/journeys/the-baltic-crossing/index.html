The Baltic Crossing

Four capitals and a sea

14 days · 5 stops · 4 countries · 646 km in a straight line

Photograph · The Baltic Crossing

The journey’s own atmosphere rather than any one of its stops: movement, or the landscape changing, or the reward at the end

The route

What the journey passes through

5 stops, in order.

⚑ History & ruins⌂ Architecture● Food◧ Design & making≈ Coast & beaches

A short, dense route round the eastern Baltic: three capitals that were occupied within living memory and one that was not, joined by ferries that locals use as buses. It is the cheapest good rail-and-boat trip in northern Europe and the least travelled by visitors from outside it.

The route, in order

Every hop is a straight-line distance between two coordinates. This atlas holds no road and no rail geometry, so that number is a floor on the leg and never the leg — Chamonix to Zermatt is 69 km here and about 170 on the ground, round a mountain range. It is here for the scale of the thing, not for planning a day.

  1. 1 Days 1–3 3 nights

    Helsinki Finland

    Granite, design, and a public sauna on the water.

  2. 2 Days 4–6 3 nights

    ↳ 82 km from Helsinki in a straight line

    Tallinn Estonia

    Two hours south by fast ferry into an intact medieval wall.

  3. 3 Days 7–9 3 nights

    ↳ 279 km from Tallinn in a straight line

    Riga Latvia

    The largest Art Nouveau quarter in Europe, and a market in five Zeppelin hangars.

  4. 4 Days 10–12 3 nights

    ↳ 262 km from Riga in a straight line

    Vilnius Lithuania

    South again: forty churches, endless courtyards, and a republic of artists.

  5. 5 Day 13 1 night

    ↳ 23 km from Vilnius in a straight line

    Trakai Lithuania

    An island castle in a lake, an hour out, to finish on.

Experiences along the way

A public sauna on the sea

Hot, then the Baltic, then hot again. The rule is that nobody performs and nobody talks much.

Helsinki · On the water

The Design District on foot

Twenty-five streets, two hundred shops and studios, and the through-line from Aalto to whoever graduated last year.

Helsinki · Walk or hike

Walk the wall, tower to tower

Two kilometres of it still stand; the sections you can climb give the roofline the postcards use.

Tallinn · Walk or hike

Bog shoes in Lahemaa

Snowshoe-like frames let you cross raised bog off the boardwalk — an hour from the capital and utterly silent.

Tallinn · Walk or hike

The facades of Alberta iela

Ninety minutes and a raised chin: everything worth seeing here is above the first floor.

Riga · Walk or hike

Lunch in the Zeppelin hangars

Smoked fish, black bread, curd and pickles bought from four different counters and eaten standing up.

Riga · Food & table

The courtyards nobody signposts

The old town’s real geography is behind the street doors; push the unlocked ones and keep going.

Vilnius · Walk or hike

Read the Užupis constitution

Forty-one articles on mirrored plaques, including a dog’s right to be a dog and everyone’s right to be happy — and not to be.

Vilnius · Walk or hike

What you will be eating

  • Finland — Rye bread, dense and sour, eaten with everything
  • Estonia — Black bread, dense and slightly sweet, served with everything
  • Latvia — Rye bread, and rye bread ice cream, which works
  • Lithuania — Cepelinai: potato dumplings the size and shape of a zeppelin

What to pack

  • A windproof layer
  • Comfortable shoes for cobbles
  • A card — the region is nearly cashless

What this estimate covers

About €1,550 per person: 13 nights at the moderate daily band for each country on the route, plus a distance-based transport figure between stops, plus 12%. It excludes getting to the start and home from the end, and it is planning arithmetic from published bands rather than a quote. How these numbers are made →
