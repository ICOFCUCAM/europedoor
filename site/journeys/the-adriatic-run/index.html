The Adriatic Run

Venice to Dubrovnik the long way round

19 days · 8 stops · 5 countries · 911 km in a straight line

≈ Coast & beaches○ Islands⚑ History & ruins● Food✝ Sacred places

Down the eastern Adriatic, which has the Mediterranean's clearest water and its least developed coastline. The route leaves the sea twice on purpose — into Slovenia's karst and into Bosnia — because the interior is where the layers are: Venetian, Ottoman, Habsburg and Yugoslav, sometimes in the same street. Ferries and buses, not trains, from Split onward.

The route, in order

Every hop is a straight-line distance between two coordinates. This atlas holds no road and no rail geometry, so that number is a floor on the leg and never the leg — Chamonix to Zermatt is 69 km here and about 170 on the ground, round a mountain range. It is here for the scale of the thing, not for planning a day.

    South to a Roman emperor's palace that people still live inside.

    ↳ 112 km from Split in a straight line

    Inland into Herzegovina, for a bridge that was destroyed and rebuilt from its own river.

  7. 7 Days 14–16 3 nights

    ↳ 129 km from Mostar in a straight line

    Kotor Montenegro

    Back to the water, at the head of a drowned canyon, with 1,350 steps up the wall behind it.

  8. 8 Days 17–18 2 nights

    ↳ 61 km from Kotor in a straight line

    Dubrovnik Croatia

    The last two hours north up the coast, into the walls. Walk them at opening.

Experiences along the way

Cicchetti and an ombra

Small plates and a small glass of wine at a bàcaro, standing, moving on after one — the Venetian version of an aperitivo.

Venice · Food & table

The palace substructures

The cellars survive because they were filled with rubbish for centuries; they map the imperial floor plan above exactly.

Split · Walk or hike

The bridge before the buses

Between six and nine the bazaar is a town rather than a market, and the bridge is a bridge.

Mostar · Walk or hike

The city walls at dawn

Ninety minutes up, the whole bay below, and a small church halfway. Go before eight or don't go in July.

Kotor · Walk or hike

What you will be eating

  • Italy — Pasta by region — trofie in Liguria, orecchiette in Puglia, carbonara only in Rome
  • Slovenia — Potica: a rolled nut cake, at every celebration
  • Croatia — Peka: meat or octopus baked under a bell with embers on top, ordered hours ahead
  • Bosnia and Herzegovina — Ćevapi: small grilled minced-meat rolls in somun bread with raw onion
  • Montenegro — Njeguški pršut, air-dried ham from a mountain village above Kotor

What to pack

  • Water shoes — most of this coast is pebble or rock
  • A dry bag for boat legs
  • Shoulders and knees covered for churches and mosques
  • Sea-sickness tablets for the open crossings

What this estimate covers
