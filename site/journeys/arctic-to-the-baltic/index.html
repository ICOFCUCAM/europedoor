Arctic to the Baltic

Start where the sun does not set and end where medieval merchants counted their money. This is the long north: an Arctic city, an island chain of granite peaks, a night train down the spine of Sweden, and then the three old trading capitals of the Baltic — each of which was rich, then occupied, and is now itself again. Long legs, and the train is the point rather than the cost.

The route

  • Days 1–3

    Tromsø · Norway

    Above the Arctic Circle: whales in winter, no darkness at all in June, and the best base in the north.

  • Days 4–7

    Lofoten (Svolvær) · Norway

    Peaks straight out of the sea and cod on racks. The one stop nobody regrets lengthening.

    ↳ 236 km — a comfortable train leg, half a day at most from Tromsø

  • Days 8–9

    Abisko · Sweden

    Over the border into Sweden's rain shadow, for the clearest skies in Lapland and the top of the Kungsleden.

    ↳ 176 km — a comfortable train leg, half a day at most from Lofoten (Svolvær)

  • Days 10–12

    Stockholm · Sweden

    The night train south delivers you to a capital on fourteen islands. Take the archipelago ferry.

    ↳ 1004 km — fly, or give the overland crossing a day of its own from Abisko

  • Days 13–14

    Helsinki · Finland

    Overnight boat across the gulf. Granite, design, and a public sauna on the water.

    ↳ 396 km — a comfortable train leg, half a day at most from Stockholm

  • Days 15–16

    Tallinn · Estonia

    Two hours south by fast ferry into an intact medieval town wall.

    ↳ 82 km — a local train or a short drive from Helsinki

  • Days 17–18

    Riga · Latvia

    The last leg by bus, into the largest Art Nouveau quarter in Europe.

    ↳ 279 km — a comfortable train leg, half a day at most from Tallinn

Experiences along the way

An aurora night with a forecast, not a promise

A guide who drives to clear sky — sometimes into Finland — is worth more than one who stays put; nobody can guarantee the lights, and anyone who does is selling something.

Tromsø · Wildlife & nature

A Sámi reindeer herding afternoon

Book with a Sámi-owned operator; the difference between a working herd and a photo backdrop is obvious within ten minutes.

Tromsø · Wildlife & nature

A night in a working rorbu

The old cod-fishing cabins on stilts; the good ones still smell faintly of tar and are still owned by fishing families.

Lofoten (Svolvær) · Walk or hike

Cold-water surf at Unstad

Beach break inside the Arctic Circle, 6mm of neoprene, and a sauna afterwards that you will remember longer than the waves.

Lofoten (Svolvær) · On the water

The first hut on the Kungsleden

One day out and one back gets you the landscape without the fortnight; the huts sell chocolate and take card.

Abisko · Walk or hike

Archipelago on a commuter boat

The Waxholmsbolaget boats are public transport that happen to cross one of the world's great island landscapes.

Stockholm · On the water

A proper fika circuit

Four bakeries, one afternoon, and the argument about whether the cardamom bun beats the cinnamon one.

Stockholm · Food & table

A public sauna on the sea

Hot, then the Baltic, then hot again. The rule is that nobody performs and nobody talks much.

Helsinki · On the water

The Design District on foot

Twenty-five streets, two hundred shops and studios, and the through-line from Aalto to whoever graduated last year.

Helsinki · Walk or hike

Walk the wall, tower to tower

Two kilometres of it still stand; the sections you can climb give the roofline the postcards use.

Tallinn · Walk or hike

What you will be eating

  • Norway — Brown cheese (brunost) — caramelised whey, sliced thin, an acquired and worthwhile taste
  • Sweden — Fika: coffee and a cardamom bun, treated as an appointment rather than a snack
  • Finland — Rye bread, dense and sour, eaten with everything
  • Estonia — Black bread, dense and slightly sweet, served with everything
  • Latvia — Rye bread, and rye bread ice cream, which works

What to pack

  • Waterproof shell and a warm layer for June — the Arctic does not read the calendar
  • Boots that will do a fell path as well as a city
  • An eye mask: it does not get dark
  • A card, not cash — the whole north is cashless
