The Alpine Grand Tour

The Alps are a single range and five nationalities, and the way to feel that is to cross them slowly by train and cable car rather than fly over. This route runs west to east from Mont Blanc to the Dolomites, staying high, and every leg is on a scheduled public service. Book the mountain huts before you book the hotels.

The route

  • Days 1–3

    Chamonix · France

    Under the highest mountain in western Europe, where alpinism was invented.

  • Days 4–6

    Zermatt · Switzerland

    Car-free, under the Matterhorn, and reached by a rack railway from the valley.

    ↳ 69 km — a local train or a short drive from Chamonix

  • Days 7–9

    Lauterbrunnen · Switzerland

    Seventy-two waterfalls, cliff-shelf villages, and the Eiger across the valley.

    ↳ 65 km — a local train or a short drive from Zermatt

  • Days 10–11

    St. Moritz & the Engadin · Switzerland

    East over the passes to the Engadin: sgraffito villages and 322 days of sun.

    ↳ 148 km — a comfortable train leg, half a day at most from Lauterbrunnen

  • Days 12–13

    Innsbruck · Austria

    The Bernina and the Arlberg deliver you to a city with a 2,300-metre mountain at the tram stop.

    ↳ 146 km — a comfortable train leg, half a day at most from St. Moritz & the Engadin

  • Days 14–16

    Ortisei & the Dolomites · Italy

    South into the Dolomites and Ladin-speaking valleys, for the pink light on limestone at dawn.

    ↳ 81 km — a local train or a short drive from Innsbruck

What to pack

  • Boots with ankle support and a broken-in sole
  • Layers, and a hat, even in August
  • Cash for mountain huts, which often take nothing else
  • Sun cream — snow at altitude burns

What this estimate covers

About €4,510 per person: 16 nights at the high daily band for each country on the route, plus a distance-based transport figure between stops, plus 12%. It excludes getting to the start and home from the end, and it is planning arithmetic from published bands rather than a quote. How these numbers are made →
