The Classical Mediterranean

Greek and Roman cities, on both shores

18 days · 6 stops · 3 countries · 1,777 km in a straight line

⚑ History & ruins≈ Coast & beaches● Food✝ Sacred places⌂ Architecture

Three thousand years of the same sea. This route takes in the best-preserved classical cities on either side of the Mediterranean — a Sicilian cathedral built inside a Doric temple, a Roman arena still hosting events, and the Anatolian coast where more of the Greek world survives than in Greece. Two flights or ferries are unavoidable and are worth it.

The route, in order

Every hop is a straight-line distance between two coordinates. This atlas holds no road and no rail geometry, so that number is a floor on the leg and never the leg — Chamonix to Zermatt is 69 km here and about 170 on the ground, round a mountain range. It is here for the scale of the thing, not for planning a day.

Experiences along the way

Testaccio, where Rome eats

The market and the streets around it are where Roman offal cooking survives; go at one, order what the sign says.

Rome · Food & table

The Appian Way on a Sunday

The road is closed to traffic on Sundays: basalt paving from 312 BC, catacombs, aqueducts and countryside, inside the city.

Rome · Walk or hike

Fried pizza on the street

Older and poorer than the baked kind, folded into a wallet and eaten walking; the sign to look for is a queue of locals.

Naples · Food & table

Palermo by street food

Panelle, arancine, sfincione, and — if you are brave — pani ca meusa. A thousand years of occupation in six snacks.

Palermo · Food & table

The Acropolis Museum, third floor

The Parthenon frieze laid out at actual scale and orientation, with plaster casts where the British Museum holds the originals — the gaps are the argument.

Athens · Museum or collection

What you will be eating

  • Italy — Pasta by region — trofie in Liguria, orecchiette in Puglia, carbonara only in Rome
  • Greece — Whatever is in the pot that day — ask to see the kitchen, which is normal
  • Türkiye — Breakfast (kahvaltı), which is a spread of fifteen small plates and takes two hours

What to pack

  • A hat and real sun protection — the sites have no shade
  • Two litres of water capacity per person
  • Boots for Ephesus and the Sicilian sites
  • Modest dress for churches and mosques

What this estimate covers
