Island Europe

An island changes the pace before you arrive, because you have to wait for a boat. These eight are chosen for being genuinely themselves rather than resort annexes — a Baltic sand spit, a Hebridean distillery island, an Adriatic island closed to outsiders until 1989, and a Cycladic island that farms.

8 places, 8 countries

Naxos

The Cyclades' agricultural heart: mountain villages, marble quarries and long beaches.

Greece

Vis

Closed as a military base until 1989 and undeveloped because of it.

Croatia

The Aran Islands (Inis Mór)

Irish as a daily language, 1,600 km of dry stone wall, and a fort on an 87-metre cliff.

Ireland

Texel

Dutch Wadden: dunes, seals, and mudflats you can walk across at low tide.

Netherlands

Islay

Nine distilleries, 3,000 people, and 50,000 wintering geese.

United Kingdom

Victoria, Gozo

Malta's slower sister, with temples older than the pyramids.

Malta

Nida, Curonian Spit

A sand spit shared with Russia, where the dunes buried four villages.

Lithuania

Lofoten (Svolvær)

Granite peaks straight out of the Norwegian Sea, above red fishing cabins.

Norway

Not an itinerary

A theme is a way of seeing, not a route: these places are not in travelling order and most people take three or four of them, not all. For an order that respects distance, put the ones you want into the Planner.
