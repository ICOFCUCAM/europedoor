Journeys where the train is the destination

Rail Europe

Some lines were built to move slate, ore or salt and are now worth the fare on their own. Others were engineering arguments. All of these are scheduled public services rather than tourist excursions, which means you can use them as transport and get the spectacle for the price of a ticket.

8 places, 7 countries

Flåm

865 metres of descent in twenty kilometres, and a gravel road to ride back down.

Norway

St. Moritz & the Engadin

The Bernina line: glacier to palm trees in four hours, over a UNESCO-listed railway.

Switzerland

Bacharach & the Rhine Gorge

The Rhine gorge line, forty castles in sixty-five kilometres, on an ordinary regional train.

Germany

Mokra Gora

The Šargan Eight: a narrow-gauge line that loops over itself to climb.

Serbia

Eryri / Snowdonia (Betws-y-Coed)

Slate railways built to run downhill by gravity, now running for the view.

United Kingdom

Pinhão & the Douro valley

The Douro line, hugging the water past terraces the whole way.

Portugal

Abisko

The Iron Ore Line to the Norwegian coast, through Lapland and above the tree line.

Sweden

Lauterbrunnen

Cog railways and cable cars as public transport, timed to the minute.

Switzerland

Not an itinerary

A theme is a way of seeing, not a route: these places are not in travelling order and most people take three or four of them, not all. For an order that respects distance, put the ones you want into the Planner.
