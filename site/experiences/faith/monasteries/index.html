Faith & heritage

Monasteries

22 across 16 countries

Austria · Belgium · Bulgaria · Croatia · Czechia · France · Georgia · Greece · Hungary · Italy · Moldova · North Macedonia · Portugal · Slovakia · Spain · United Kingdom

  1. Vienna, Austria · Cellar & vineyard · moderate

    A heuriger on the city edge

    Wine taverns in Vienna's own vineyards, open only when the grower has wine to sell — a pine branch over the door means open.

  2. Brussels, Belgium · Cellar & vineyard · moderate

    A lambic brewery, spontaneously fermented

    Beer left open to the air of one valley, aged three years in barrel — the last place in the world doing this at scale.

  3. Rila Monastery, Bulgaria · Sacred site · low

    A night in a monastery cell

    Simple rooms, an early curfew, and the courtyard entirely to yourself at dawn.

  4. Split, Croatia · Walk or hike · low

    The palace substructures

    The cellars survive because they were filled with rubbish for centuries; they map the imperial floor plan above exactly.

  5. Mikulov, Czechia · Cellar & vineyard · low

    A Moravian cellar at harvest

    Burčák — half-fermented, cloudy and briefly available each September — is drunk cold from the barrel and does not travel.

  6. Avignon, France · Cellar & vineyard · moderate

    Châteauneuf-du-Pape, in the village

    Thirteen permitted grape varieties and stones the size of fists that hold the day's heat into the night; the growers pour in their own cellars.

  7. Bordeaux, France · Cellar & vineyard · moderate

    A small Saint-Émilion cellar

    Skip the classified growths and visit a family making 20,000 bottles; you will taste from the barrel and be told the truth about the vintage.

  8. Reims, France · Cellar & vineyard · moderate

    Down into the crayères

    Chalk quarried by Romans, thirty metres down, holding millions of bottles at a constant temperature nobody has to pay for.

  9. Sighnaghi & Kakheti, Georgia · Cellar & vineyard · low

    A qvevri cellar and a supra

    Wine fermented in clay buried to the neck, then a table that goes on for hours with a tamada running it.

  10. Meteora (Kalambaka), Greece · Walk or hike · low

    The old monks' paths

    Stone-cut trails link the pillars through the woods; three hours on foot beats a whole day by road.

  11. Santorini, Greece · Cellar & vineyard · moderate

    Vines grown in baskets

    No trellises: the vines are woven into circles on the ground to shelter the fruit from wind and catch night moisture.

  12. Tokaj, Hungary · Cellar & vineyard · low

    A mouldy cellar in Tokaj

    The black fungus on the walls regulates humidity and is left alone deliberately; taste dry furmint first and six-puttonyos last.

  13. Assisi, Italy · Sacred site · low

    Up to the hermitage on foot

    An hour uphill through holm oak to the caves Francis actually used, which are quieter and more convincing than the basilica.

  14. Turin, Italy · Cellar & vineyard · low

    Vermouth where it was invented

    Carpano started here in 1786. The historic cafés still serve it the way it was drunk: cold, alone, with a small plate.

  15. Chișinău, Moldova · Cellar & vineyard · low

    Driving through a wine cellar

    200 km of limestone galleries with street names, and two million bottles. Visitors go in by vehicle because walking would take days.

  16. Ohrid, North Macedonia · On the water · low

    Across the lake to Sveti Naum

    An hour and a half by boat to a monastery at the springs on the Albanian border, past cliffs with nothing else on them.

  17. Pinhão & the Douro valley, Portugal · Cellar & vineyard · moderate

    Treading grapes at harvest

    Knee-deep in a granite tank for two hours, in a line, to a rhythm — the least romantic and most enjoyable part of winemaking.

  18. Porto, Portugal · Cellar & vineyard · moderate

    Port, tasted in the right order

    White, then tawny by age, then vintage — the tawnies are where the interest is and where the guides usually rush.

  19. Évora, Portugal · Cellar & vineyard · low

    Cork oaks and Alentejo reds

    The trees are stripped every nine years and the date is painted on the trunk; the wine estates between them charge a fraction of the Douro.

  20. Bratislava, Slovakia · Cellar & vineyard · low

    The Small Carpathian wine road

    Twenty minutes from the capital: family cellars in Pezinok and Modra that sell almost nothing beyond the region.

  21. Ribeira Sacra, Spain · Cellar & vineyard · moderate

    Heroic viticulture, from the river

    The growers call it viticultura heroica without irony: everything is carried up and down by hand or cable, and the boat is the only way to see why.

  22. Islay, United Kingdom · Cellar & vineyard · moderate

    Three distilleries on foot

    Ardbeg, Lagavulin and Laphroaig sit within five kilometres of each other along one coastal path — walk it, for obvious reasons.

Selected by name and description against monaster, hermitage, cell and monk — matched on what we wrote about the experience, never on the name of the town. 4 more terms are declared for this list and have matched nothing yet: abbey, convent, tekke, lavra. That is a gap in the writing rather than a fact about Europe.
