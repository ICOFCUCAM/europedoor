8 experiences across 8 countries.

A heuriger on the city edge

Wine taverns in Vienna's own vineyards, open only when the grower has wine to sell — a pine branch over the door means open.

Vienna, Austria · moderate

The old monks' paths

Stone-cut trails link the pillars through the woods; three hours on foot beats a whole day by road.

Meteora (Kalambaka), Greece · low

Whales from an old oak boat

The refitted fishing schooners cut their engines and drift; the silence is half of what you came for.

Húsavík, Iceland · moderate

Up to the hermitage on foot

An hour uphill through holm oak to the caves Francis actually used, which are quieter and more convincing than the basilica.

Assisi, Italy · low

Bison at first light

A guide, a hide and a 4 am start; the forest's edge meadows are where the herds actually come out.

Białowieża, Poland · moderate

Cork oaks and Alentejo reds

The trees are stripped every nine years and the date is painted on the trunk; the wine estates between them charge a fraction of the Douro.

Évora, Portugal · low

A section of the Lycian Way

Waymarked in red and white since 1999, through pine, ruins and goat country, with village pensions at the ends of most stages.

Fethiye & the Lycian coast, Türkiye · low

The slate railway to the coast

Built in 1836 to run loaded downhill by gravity; it still descends 700 feet to Porthmadog through the woods.

Eryri / Snowdonia (Betws-y-Coed), United Kingdom · moderate
