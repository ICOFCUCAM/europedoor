How this list is built

Anything in a place tagged art, music, design, festivals, architecture, plus anything whose own name or description matches one of the 46 terms under the sub-categories below.

The Koman Lake ferry

Three hours through a flooded gorge on a working passenger boat that carries villagers, goats and the occasional car.

Theth, Albania · low

Bunk'Art, underground

A five-storey bunker built for a leadership expecting invasion that never came; the exhibition does not soften the regime.

Tirana, Albania · low

Chant in a rock-cut chamber

Singers perform in the carved chambers because the acoustics are extraordinary; the sound arrives from the stone itself.

Garni & Geghard, Armenia · low

The manuscript library

Illuminated books rescued repeatedly over centuries, some buried during massacres and dug up afterwards.

Yerevan, Armenia · low

Into the salt mountain

Miners' slides, a salt lake underground, and the Bronze Age tools that came out of the same shafts.

Hallstatt, Austria · moderate

City to summit in twenty minutes

Tram, funicular and two cable cars take you from a café table to 2,256 metres of exposed ridge; take a jacket you would not think you needed.

Innsbruck, Austria · moderate

Standing room at the Staatsoper

A few euros, queued for on the night, in one of the great opera houses. Bring a scarf to tie to the rail and hold your place.

Vienna, Austria · low

A heuriger on the city edge

Wine taverns in Vienna's own vineyards, open only when the grower has wine to sell — a pine branch over the door means open.

Vienna, Austria · moderate

Petroglyphs and mud volcanoes

Rock carvings of boats and dancers from 10,000 years ago, then a field of cold mud volcanoes burping methane a few kilometres on.

Baku, Azerbaijan · moderate

Shebeke: glass without glue

Wooden lattice and coloured glass assembled entirely by friction; the workshops still make them and will show you the joints.

Sheki, Azerbaijan · low

Bruges before the coaches

Day-trippers arrive from ten. Between six and nine the canals belong to you and the swans.

Bruges, Belgium · low

A lambic brewery, spontaneously fermented

Beer left open to the air of one valley, aged three years in barrel — the last place in the world doing this at scale.

Brussels, Belgium · moderate

The bridge before the buses

Between six and nine the bazaar is a town rather than a market, and the bridge is a bridge.

Mostar, Bosnia and Herzegovina · low

The Tunnel of Hope

800 metres dug by hand under the runway; it kept the city alive for three years and is explained by people who used it.

Sarajevo, Bosnia and Herzegovina · low

A concert in a Roman theatre

Built for 6,000 in the 2nd century, rediscovered by a landslide in 1972, and used most summer evenings.

Plovdiv, Bulgaria · moderate

Plitvice at opening

Timed entry tickets sell out; the 7 am slot gives you an hour of empty boardwalk before the coaches from the coast arrive.

Plitvice Lakes, Croatia · moderate

The palace substructures

The cellars survive because they were filled with rubbish for centuries; they map the imperial floor plan above exactly.

Split, Croatia · low

Peka in a village konoba

Ordered by phone that morning: octopus and potatoes under an iron bell buried in embers for three hours.

Vis, Croatia · moderate

The barn-roofed painted churches

Small stone chapels under enormous pitched roofs, frescoed inside floor to ceiling; several need a key fetched from a village house.

Omodos & the wine villages, Cyprus · low

A Moravian cellar at harvest

Burčák — half-fermented, cloudy and briefly available each September — is drunk cold from the barrel and does not travel.

Mikulov, Czechia · low

A pub with a tapster, not a menu

The good places pour continuously and mark your tab in pencil; you say when to stop, and the pour type is a real choice.

Prague, Czechia · low

Vyšehrad and the river bend

The city's first castle, a cemetery of composers, and the whole of Prague from the ramparts without a crowd.

Prague, Czechia · low

Walk the rainbow

150 metres of coloured glass on the roof; the city changes hue as you walk, which is the entire idea.

Aarhus, Denmark · moderate

Swim in the harbour

Free, supervised in summer, and the result of thirty years of cleaning up a working port.

Copenhagen, Denmark · low

Smørrebrød in the correct order

Herring first, then fish, then meat, then cheese, with aquavit between — a lunch that takes three hours and is meant to.

Copenhagen, Denmark · moderate

Row a reconstructed longship

Built with period tools from the excavated hulls; you supply the arms and learn quickly why Vikings were fit.

Roskilde, Denmark · moderate

Walk the wall, tower to tower

Two kilometres of it still stand; the sections you can climb give the roofline the postcards use.

Tallinn, Estonia · low

Bog shoes in Lahemaa

Snowshoe-like frames let you cross raised bog off the boardwalk — an hour from the capital and utterly silent.

Tallinn, Estonia · moderate

A public sauna on the sea

Hot, then the Baltic, then hot again. The rule is that nobody performs and nobody talks much.

Helsinki, Finland · moderate

The Design District on foot

Twenty-five streets, two hundred shops and studios, and the through-line from Aalto to whoever graduated last year.

Helsinki, Finland · low

Siida, on Sámi terms

A national minority telling its own story, including the parts about the Finnish state — worth an afternoon rather than an hour.

Inari, Finland · low

Looking for the Saimaa seal

Fewer than four hundred exist. A quiet boat, an early morning, and no promises — which is how it should be sold.

Savonlinna, Finland · moderate

Château to château by bicycle

900 km of signed, flat riverside route; two days of it between Blois and Tours is the best introduction.

Amboise & the Loire châteaux, France · moderate

The Camargue from a horse

The local white horses are bred to work in water; a half-day gets you into marsh no vehicle reaches.

Arles, France · moderate

Châteauneuf-du-Pape, in the village

Thirteen permitted grape varieties and stones the size of fists that hold the day's heat into the night; the growers pour in their own cellars.

Avignon, France · moderate

A small Saint-Émilion cellar

Skip the classified growths and visit a family making 20,000 bottles; you will taste from the barrel and be told the truth about the vintage.

Bordeaux, France · moderate

Walk into the Calanques

From Luminy to Sugiton in ninety minutes; in summer access is capped and booked, which is why the water is still clear.

Marseille, France · low

Cross the bay on foot

Guided, barefoot, over quicksand and channels; the mount is a different thing entirely when you arrive the medieval way.

Mont-Saint-Michel, France · moderate

A market street before ten

Cheese, bread, a rotisserie chicken and a bottle, then eat it in a park; the most Parisian thing available and it costs almost nothing.

Paris, France · low

The covered passages

Glass-roofed arcades built before the department store and mostly forgotten since — a two-hour walk in the dry.

Paris, France · low

Down into the crayères

Chalk quarried by Romans, thirty metres down, holding millions of bottles at a constant temperature nobody has to pay for.

Reims, France · moderate

The painted caves, original and copy

Font-de-Gaume still admits a few dozen people a day to originals; Lascaux is a facsimile because breath destroyed the real one within fifteen years of opening.

Sarlat & the Dordogne, France · moderate

Mestia to Ushguli on foot

Four days between villages with guesthouses each night, no need to carry a tent, and glaciers in view most of the way.

Mestia, Svaneti, Georgia · moderate

A qvevri cellar and a supra

Wine fermented in clay buried to the neck, then a table that goes on for hours with a tamada running it.

Sighnaghi & Kakheti, Georgia · low

A private room at the sulphur baths

Hot spring water at 40°C, a hard scrub if you want one, and an hour that costs less than a meal.

Tbilisi, Georgia · low

The Wall, where it stood

Bernauer Straße preserves the death strip, the tunnels and the names — an hour that does what no film manages.

Berlin, Germany · low

Cycle the runways at Tempelhof

Two 2,000-metre runways, open to anyone, kept deliberately empty by referendum.

Berlin, Germany · low

The 62 ferry, not the tour boat

A public transport ferry down the Elbe past the container terminals; same view, a fraction of the price.

Hamburg, Germany · low

A beer garden, done correctly

Buy the beer, bring the food, sit at the unmarked communal tables — the marked ones are for served customers and the distinction matters.

Munich, Germany · low

The Acropolis Museum, third floor

The Parthenon frieze laid out at actual scale and orientation, with plaster casts where the British Museum holds the originals — the gaps are the argument.

Athens, Greece · moderate

A panigiri that ends at dawn

A village saint's day: one long table, live violin and lute, dancing in a circle, and food priced to cover the church roof.

Ikaria, Greece · low

An outdoor thermal bath in winter

38°C water, snow on the stone, and old men playing chess on floating boards. Go early; go on a weekday.

Budapest, Hungary · moderate

A ruin bar, before it fills

Derelict courtyard buildings filled with salvaged furniture; Szimpla was the first and is still worth an early evening.

Budapest, Hungary · low

The local pool, not the lagoon

Every district has one: hot pots at different temperatures, a strict pre-shower rule, and the day's arguments happening in them.

Reykjavík, Iceland · low

A real session, not a show

Musicians sit in a circle facing each other, not the room; the etiquette is quiet during tunes and buy the round.

Dublin, Ireland · low

Oysters at the counter

Native flat oysters from the bay outside, September to April; a dozen, brown bread, and no ceremony.

Galway, Ireland · moderate

The island by bicycle

Hire at the pier, ride the length of it, and be back for the last ferry — the island's whole scale in one day.

The Aran Islands (Inis Mór), Ireland · low

Inside a nuraghe

Su Nuraxi at Barumini is the UNESCO one, but there are seven thousand of these dry-stone towers and you can walk into many of them alone.

Alghero, Sardinia, Italy · low

Up to the hermitage on foot

An hour uphill through holm oak to the caves Francis actually used, which are quieter and more convincing than the basilica.

Assisi, Italy · low

The 666 arches to San Luca

Four kilometres of covered walkway climbing out of the city, built so a procession would never get wet.

Bologna, Italy · low

Vespers at San Miniato

Gregorian chant in an 11th-century church above the city, at 17:30, free, and almost always uncrowded.

Florence, Italy · low

Round the walls on a bicycle

Four kilometres of Renaissance rampart, planted with plane trees in 1818 and used as a promenade ever since.

Lucca, Italy · low

Fried pizza on the street

Older and poorer than the baked kind, folded into a wallet and eaten walking; the sign to look for is a queue of locals.

Naples, Italy · low

A night in a rifugio

Dinner at seven, lights out at ten, and the Enrosadira — the pink glow on the rock at dawn — from the door.

Ortisei & the Dolomites, Italy · moderate

Palermo by street food

Panelle, arancine, sfincione, and — if you are brave — pani ca meusa. A thousand years of occupation in six snacks.

Palermo, Italy · low

Testaccio, where Rome eats

The market and the streets around it are where Roman offal cooking survives; go at one, order what the sign says.

Rome, Italy · low

The Appian Way on a Sunday

The road is closed to traffic on Sundays: basalt paving from 312 BC, catacombs, aqueducts and countryside, inside the city.

Rome, Italy · low

Vermouth where it was invented

Carpano started here in 1786. The historic cafés still serve it the way it was drunk: cold, alone, with a small plate.

Turin, Italy · low

Cicchetti and an ombra

Small plates and a small glass of wine at a bàcaro, standing, moving on after one — the Venetian version of an aperitivo.

Venice, Italy · low

The terraces, from above

The high paths behind the villages are free, empty and better; the coastal one is the queue.

Vernazza & the Cinque Terre, Italy · moderate

Opera in a Roman arena

Fifteen thousand people, no amplification, and the cheap seats on the stone steps are the ones with the acoustics.

Verona, Italy · moderate

The Peaks of the Balkans trail

192 km across three countries in ten days, with village guesthouses and border permits arranged in advance.

Peja & Rugova, Kosovo · moderate

Up to Kaljaja at sunset

Twenty minutes of steps for the whole town, its minarets and its bell towers in one frame.

Prizren, Kosovo · low

The facades of Alberta iela

Ninety minutes and a raised chin: everything worth seeing here is above the first floor.

Riga, Latvia · low

Lunch in the Zeppelin hangars

Smoked fish, black bread, curd and pickles bought from four different counters and eaten standing up.

Riga, Latvia · low

A day on the Liechtenstein Trail

75 km through all eleven municipalities; the Vaduz-to-Balzers section takes an afternoon and crosses a quarter of the country.

Vaduz, Liechtenstein · low

The grey dunes at first light

Sand that buried four villages in the 19th century; the boardwalk exists because walking on it directly would start it moving again.

Nida, Curonian Spit, Lithuania · low

The courtyards nobody signposts

The old town's real geography is behind the street doors; push the unlocked ones and keep going.

Vilnius, Lithuania · low

Read the Užupis constitution

Forty-one articles on mirrored plaques, including a dog's right to be a dog and everyone's right to be happy — and not to be.

Vilnius, Lithuania · low

Into the Bock casemates

Kilometres of gun galleries in the cliff; they sheltered 35,000 people in 1944 and are cold in any weather.

Luxembourg City, Luxembourg · low

Across the Grand Harbour by dgħajsa

A rowed and now motorised water taxi in the old harbour shape; the cheapest and best view of the bastions.

Valletta, Malta · low

The Xwejni salt pans

Rock-cut pans worked by the same family for five generations; they harvest by hand in summer and will explain it if you ask.

Victoria, Gozo, Malta · low

Walk the Grand Prix circuit

3.3 km of ordinary public road: the tunnel, the hairpin, the harbour chicane. It takes forty minutes and recalibrates what you think those cars are doing.

Monaco-Ville, Monaco · low

The city walls at dawn

Ninety minutes up, the whole bay below, and a small church halfway. Go before eight or don't go in July.

Kotor, Montenegro · low

The city the way it is used

Rent for a day, ride out to Amsterdam-Noord on the free ferry, and stop thinking of the bicycle as an activity.

Amsterdam, Netherlands · low

The Old Bazaar at working hours

Coppersmiths, filigree, tea houses and mosques — a working market rather than a preserved one, best before noon.

Skopje, North Macedonia · low

Six thousand years of rock carving

Thousands of figures — boats, reindeer, bears, people — pecked into coastal rock over five millennia, and read in order along a boardwalk.

Alta, Norway · low

Fløyen to Ulriken ridge walk

Five hours along the tops between the two cable cars, with the city on one side and nothing but bog and rock on the other.

Bergen, Norway · low

Lunch at a working fish counter

Skip the tourist stalls; eat where the boats deliver, standing up, whatever came in that morning.

Bergen, Norway · moderate

Saltstraumen at full flow

Check the tide table and stand on the bridge: whirlpools ten metres across, and fishermen working the edge of them.

Bodø, Norway · low

The free ferry, all day

Fredrikstad runs its river crossings free of charge as ordinary transport; ride them between the old town and the new for as long as you like.

Fredrikstad, Norway · low

Maihaugen open-air museum

Two hundred buildings moved log by log from across Gudbrandsdalen; the point is the carpentry, not the costumes.

Lillehammer, Norway · moderate

Cold-water surf at Unstad

Beach break inside the Arctic Circle, 6mm of neoprene, and a sauna afterwards that you will remember longer than the waves.

Lofoten (Svolvær), Norway · moderate

The commuter ferry as a day out

Ride the public boats to Hovedøya and Gressholmen with a swim towel and a bakery bag; it is a city transport ticket, not a cruise.

Oslo, Norway · low

Waterfront architecture walk

Opera roof to Munch to the library at Bjørvika — twenty years of a city deciding what it wanted to look like, in ninety minutes.

Oslo, Norway · low

The wooden boat festival

Every August the harbour fills with clinker-built boats and the people who maintain them; go for the sound of the hulls rather than the programme.

Risør, Norway · moderate

Vemork and the heavy water raid

The plant, the pipeline the saboteurs climbed, and an account that does not tidy up how close it came to failing.

Rjukan, Norway · moderate

Down the Olav mine

Five degrees underground year-round, hard hats, and the noise a hand drill made before compressed air.

Røros, Norway · moderate

An aurora night with a forecast, not a promise

A guide who drives to clear sky — sometimes into Finland — is worth more than one who stays put; nobody can guarantee the lights, and anyone who does is selling something.

Tromsø, Norway · high

A Sámi reindeer herding afternoon

Book with a Sámi-owned operator; the difference between a working herd and a photo backdrop is obvious within ten minutes.

Tromsø, Norway · moderate

The cathedral, from the workshop side

The stoneyard has been cutting replacement blocks continuously for a century; see that before you see the nave.

Trondheim, Norway · low

The town that burned and matched

A short walk that only works once you know the date: every façade you pass was drawn in the same three years.

Ålesund, Norway · low

Lunch in a milk bar

A communist-era subsidised canteen, still running, still cheap; queue, point, pay, and eat what Poland actually eats.

Kraków, Poland · low

The salt cathedral at Wieliczka

A chapel 101 metres underground with chandeliers of salt crystal, carved by miners over decades in their own time.

Kraków, Poland · moderate

POLIN, a thousand years

The museum deliberately spends most of its space on Jewish life rather than its destruction, which is what makes it unlike any other.

Warsaw, Poland · moderate

Fado in a small house

Lights down, conversation stops, and two guitars. The venues that ask for silence are the ones worth finding.

Lisbon, Portugal · moderate

Azulejos, street by street

Tiled facades were cheap weatherproofing before they were beautiful; the Tile Museum explains it and then the streets deliver it.

Lisbon, Portugal · low

Treading grapes at harvest

Knee-deep in a granite tank for two hours, in a line, to a rhythm — the least romantic and most enjoyable part of winemaking.

Pinhão & the Douro valley, Portugal · moderate

Port, tasted in the right order

White, then tawny by age, then vintage — the tawnies are where the interest is and where the guides usually rush.

Porto, Portugal · moderate

A bear hide in the Carpathians

Licensed hides run by biologists at dusk; Romania holds most of Europe's brown bears and this is the ethical way to see one.

Brașov, Romania · moderate

The blue at Voroneț

A 1547 exterior fresco whose blue has not faded in five hundred winters, and whose recipe is still not known.

Gura Humorului & the painted monasteries, Romania · low

Into the delta by small boat

The big tour boats stay in the main channels; a local with a small engine gets you into the reed corridors where the birds are.

Tulcea & the Danube Delta, Romania · moderate

Haymaking with a scythe

The meadows are cut by hand because machines would destroy them; in June the villages will show you how and it is harder than it looks.

Viscri, Romania · low

The witches' path between the towers

A narrow ridge path with drops on both sides, linking Guaita and Cesta; twenty minutes, and the whole reason the republic survived.

City of San Marino, San Marino · low

A kafana with live music

Long tables, a small band moving between them, rakija, and no fixed end time.

Belgrade, Serbia · low

The grid, read as a plan

Cerdà's 1859 blocks were designed with gardens inside; a few have been reopened, and finding them changes how the city reads.

Barcelona, Spain · low

Across on the hanging gondola

The world's oldest transporter bridge, on the UNESCO list, still running every eight minutes as a local crossing.

Bilbao, Spain · low

The Camí de Ronda coastal path

A smugglers' and coastguards' path linking coves between Begur and Palamós; it reaches beaches no road does.

Girona & the Empordà, Spain · low

The Nasrid palaces, on a timed ticket

Half-hour entry slots, sold out weeks ahead. Take the earliest of the day — the courtyards were designed for low light and few people.

Granada, Spain · moderate

Tapas, the Granada way

Every drink comes with a plate you did not order and cannot choose; four bars is a dinner.

Granada, Spain · low

La hora del vermut

Sunday, around one, house vermouth on tap with an olive and a siphon of soda — the most Madrid ninety minutes there is.

Madrid, Spain · low

Classical drama in a Roman theatre

Greek and Roman plays staged every July and August in the theatre they were written for, which is a different experience from reading them.

Mérida, Spain · moderate

The wooden train to Sóller

1912 rolling stock through thirteen tunnels and an orange valley, then a tram to the port. Ordinary transport, and the best hour on the island.

Palma de Mallorca, Spain · moderate

The last hundred kilometres

From Sarria, five days, and the minimum distance for a compostela. Busy, sociable, and still the real thing.

Santiago de Compostela, Spain · low

Flamenco in a peña

Members' clubs that admit visitors quietly; no castanets, no stage, and the singing is the part that matters.

Seville, Spain · moderate

Paella where the rice grows

The Albufera lagoon south of the city grows the rice and cooks it over orange wood; lunchtime only, and never in the evening.

Valencia, Spain · moderate

The open-air cold bathhouse

A wooden pier, a sauna, and the Öresund at four degrees; the locals do it in February and talk about it all year.

Malmö, Sweden · low

Archipelago on a commuter boat

The Waxholmsbolaget boats are public transport that happen to cross one of the world's great island landscapes.

Stockholm, Sweden · low

A proper fika circuit

Four bakeries, one afternoon, and the argument about whether the cardamom bun beats the cinnamon one.

Stockholm, Sweden · low

Linnaeus's garden, in his order

The beds are laid out by his sexual system of classification, which is the point and also the scandal of 1735.

Uppsala, Sweden · low

Float the Aare

Walk upstream, get in, and let the river carry you back through the city — with a dry bag and a firm plan for getting out.

Bern, Switzerland · low

The Bernina line to Tirano

From glacier to palm trees in four hours, over the Landwasser viaduct and around the Brusio spiral — booked as a normal train, not a tour.

St. Moritz & the Engadin, Switzerland · moderate

A section of the Lycian Way

Waymarked in red and white since 1999, through pine, ruins and goat country, with village pensions at the ends of most stages.

Fethiye & the Lycian coast, Türkiye · low

Between continents on a commuter ferry

Twenty minutes, a glass of tea, gulls following the stern, and Europe and Asia both in view. The single best thing in the city and nearly free.

Istanbul, Türkiye · low

A two-hour Turkish breakfast

Fifteen small dishes, endless tea, and no expectation that you leave. Order for the table and clear the morning.

Istanbul, Türkiye · low

The walls, told by both sides

The good operators take you to both communities and let the contradictions stand rather than resolving them for you.

Belfast, United Kingdom · moderate

The last day of the Pilgrims' Way

Twenty miles from Chilham along the North Downs, arriving on foot the way it was meant to be done.

Canterbury, United Kingdom · low

Up Arthur's Seat before breakfast

251 metres of extinct volcano, forty minutes up, and the whole city and the Forth laid out at the top.

Edinburgh, United Kingdom · low

The slate railway to the coast

Built in 1836 to run loaded downhill by gravity; it still descends 700 feet to Porthmadog through the woods.

Eryri / Snowdonia (Betws-y-Coed), United Kingdom · moderate

Three distilleries on foot

Ardbeg, Lagavulin and Laphroaig sit within five kilometres of each other along one coastal path — walk it, for obvious reasons.

Islay, United Kingdom · moderate

The Quiraing circuit

Seven kilometres through a landscape still slowly moving downhill; start early, because the car park fills by nine.

Isle of Skye (Portree), United Kingdom · low

A Wainwright, start to finish

214 fells were catalogued by one man in seven hand-written volumes; walking one of the small ones is the best introduction to why.

Keswick & the Lakes, United Kingdom · low

The Hampstead bathing ponds

Three ponds, open every day of the year, including the January mornings when the ice is broken by hand.

London, United Kingdom · low

One free national collection a day

Britain's museums charge nothing for the permanent collections; treat one as an hour's visit rather than a day's endurance.

London, United Kingdom · low

A day on the South West Coast Path

1,014 kilometres in total; the St Ives-to-Zennor stretch gives you the best of it in six hours.

St Ives, United Kingdom · low

Up the dome of St Peter's

The staircase follows the curve of the dome and tilts with it; the reward is the whole of Rome and the gardens nobody else sees.

Vatican City, Vatican City · low

The scavi, under the basilica

A Roman street of tombs beneath the church, ending at what may be St Peter's grave. Twelve people at a time, booked by email, months ahead.

Vatican City, Vatican City · moderate

One door into Europe — discover it, plan it, and leave it better than you found it.
