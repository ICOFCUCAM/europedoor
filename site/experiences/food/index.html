Food & drink

Markets, tables, cellars, vineyards and the dishes that belong to one valley.

  1. A heuriger on the city edge

    Wine taverns in Vienna's own vineyards, open only when the grower has wine to sell — a pine branch over the door means open.

    Vienna, Austria · Cellar & vineyard · moderate

  2. A lambic brewery, spontaneously fermented

    Beer left open to the air of one valley, aged three years in barrel — the last place in the world doing this at scale.

    Brussels, Belgium · Cellar & vineyard · moderate

  3. The bridge before the buses

    Between six and nine the bazaar is a town rather than a market, and the bridge is a bridge.

    Mostar, Bosnia and Herzegovina · Walk or hike · low

  4. Bosnian coffee, properly

    Džezva, fildžan, a sugar cube held in the teeth, and an hour of not being in a hurry.

    Sarajevo, Bosnia and Herzegovina · Food & table · low

  5. The palace substructures

    The cellars survive because they were filled with rubbish for centuries; they map the imperial floor plan above exactly.

    Split, Croatia · Walk or hike · low

  6. Peka in a village konoba

    Ordered by phone that morning: octopus and potatoes under an iron bell buried in embers for three hours.

    Vis, Croatia · Food & table · moderate

  7. A Moravian cellar at harvest

    Burčák — half-fermented, cloudy and briefly available each September — is drunk cold from the barrel and does not travel.

    Mikulov, Czechia · Cellar & vineyard · low

  8. A pub with a tapster, not a menu

    The good places pour continuously and mark your tab in pencil; you say when to stop, and the pour type is a real choice.

    Prague, Czechia · Food & table · low

  9. Smørrebrød in the correct order

    Herring first, then fish, then meat, then cheese, with aquavit between — a lunch that takes three hours and is meant to.

    Copenhagen, Denmark · Food & table · moderate

  10. Châteauneuf-du-Pape, in the village

    Thirteen permitted grape varieties and stones the size of fists that hold the day's heat into the night; the growers pour in their own cellars.

    Avignon, France · Cellar & vineyard · moderate

  11. A small Saint-Émilion cellar

    Skip the classified growths and visit a family making 20,000 bottles; you will taste from the barrel and be told the truth about the vintage.

    Bordeaux, France · Cellar & vineyard · moderate

  12. A market street before ten

    Cheese, bread, a rotisserie chicken and a bottle, then eat it in a park; the most Parisian thing available and it costs almost nothing.

    Paris, France · Food & table · low

  13. Down into the crayères

    Chalk quarried by Romans, thirty metres down, holding millions of bottles at a constant temperature nobody has to pay for.

    Reims, France · Cellar & vineyard · moderate

  14. A qvevri cellar and a supra

    Wine fermented in clay buried to the neck, then a table that goes on for hours with a tamada running it.

    Sighnaghi & Kakheti, Georgia · Cellar & vineyard · low

  15. Above the vineyards on the Rheinsteig

    The high path gives you the whole gorge; the trains and boats below give you the way back.

    Bacharach & the Rhine Gorge, Germany · Walk or hike · low

  16. A beer garden, done correctly

    Buy the beer, bring the food, sit at the unmarked communal tables — the marked ones are for served customers and the distinction matters.

    Munich, Germany · Food & table · low

  17. The interior, not the beach

    Half the island is farmland at altitude — cheese, potatoes and Venetian towers, twenty minutes from the sand.

    Naxos, Greece · Rail, road or ride · low

  18. Vines grown in baskets

    No trellises: the vines are woven into circles on the ground to shelter the fruit from wind and catch night moisture.

    Santorini, Greece · Cellar & vineyard · moderate

  19. A mouldy cellar in Tokaj

    The black fungus on the walls regulates humidity and is left alone deliberately; taste dry furmint first and six-puttonyos last.

    Tokaj, Hungary · Cellar & vineyard · low

  20. Oysters at the counter

    Native flat oysters from the bay outside, September to April; a dozen, brown bread, and no ceremony.

    Galway, Ireland · Food & table · moderate

  21. Fried pizza on the street

    Older and poorer than the baked kind, folded into a wallet and eaten walking; the sign to look for is a queue of locals.

    Naples, Italy · Food & table · low

  22. A night in a rifugio

    Dinner at seven, lights out at ten, and the Enrosadira — the pink glow on the rock at dawn — from the door.

    Ortisei & the Dolomites, Italy · Walk or hike · moderate

  23. Palermo by street food

    Panelle, arancine, sfincione, and — if you are brave — pani ca meusa. A thousand years of occupation in six snacks.

    Palermo, Italy · Food & table · low

  24. Testaccio, where Rome eats

    The market and the streets around it are where Roman offal cooking survives; go at one, order what the sign says.

    Rome, Italy · Food & table · low

  25. Vermouth where it was invented

    Carpano started here in 1786. The historic cafés still serve it the way it was drunk: cold, alone, with a small plate.

    Turin, Italy · Cellar & vineyard · low

  26. Cicchetti and an ombra

    Small plates and a small glass of wine at a bàcaro, standing, moving on after one — the Venetian version of an aperitivo.

    Venice, Italy · Food & table · low

  27. Lunch in the Zeppelin hangars

    Smoked fish, black bread, curd and pickles bought from four different counters and eaten standing up.

    Riga, Latvia · Food & table · low

  28. The Xwejni salt pans

    Rock-cut pans worked by the same family for five generations; they harvest by hand in summer and will explain it if you ask.

    Victoria, Gozo, Malta · Workshop or making · low

  29. Driving through a wine cellar

    200 km of limestone galleries with street names, and two million bottles. Visitors go in by vehicle because walking would take days.

    Chișinău, Moldova · Cellar & vineyard · low

  30. The Old Bazaar at working hours

    Coppersmiths, filigree, tea houses and mosques — a working market rather than a preserved one, best before noon.

    Skopje, North Macedonia · Walk or hike · low

  31. Lunch at a working fish counter

    Skip the tourist stalls; eat where the boats deliver, standing up, whatever came in that morning.

    Bergen, Norway · Food & table · moderate

  32. The commuter ferry as a day out

    Ride the public boats to Hovedøya and Gressholmen with a swim towel and a bakery bag; it is a city transport ticket, not a cruise.

    Oslo, Norway · On the water · low

  33. Lunch in a milk bar

    A communist-era subsidised canteen, still running, still cheap; queue, point, pay, and eat what Poland actually eats.

    Kraków, Poland · Food & table · low

  34. Treading grapes at harvest

    Knee-deep in a granite tank for two hours, in a line, to a rhythm — the least romantic and most enjoyable part of winemaking.

    Pinhão & the Douro valley, Portugal · Cellar & vineyard · moderate

  35. Port, tasted in the right order

    White, then tawny by age, then vintage — the tawnies are where the interest is and where the guides usually rush.

    Porto, Portugal · Cellar & vineyard · moderate

  36. Cork oaks and Alentejo reds

    The trees are stripped every nine years and the date is painted on the trunk; the wine estates between them charge a fraction of the Douro.

    Évora, Portugal · Cellar & vineyard · low

  37. A kafana with live music

    Long tables, a small band moving between them, rakija, and no fixed end time.

    Belgrade, Serbia · Music & performance · low

  38. The Small Carpathian wine road

    Twenty minutes from the capital: family cellars in Pezinok and Modra that sell almost nothing beyond the region.

    Bratislava, Slovakia · Cellar & vineyard · low

  39. Hut to hut in the High Tatras

    Beds, soup and beer at 1,500 metres, carried up by porters on wooden frames — a practice still going and worth watching.

    Poprad & the High Tatras, Slovakia · Walk or hike · moderate

  40. Tapas, the Granada way

    Every drink comes with a plate you did not order and cannot choose; four bars is a dinner.

    Granada, Spain · Food & table · low

  41. La hora del vermut

    Sunday, around one, house vermouth on tap with an olive and a siphon of soda — the most Madrid ninety minutes there is.

    Madrid, Spain · Food & table · low

  42. Heroic viticulture, from the river

    The growers call it viticultura heroica without irony: everything is carried up and down by hand or cable, and the boat is the only way to see why.

    Ribeira Sacra, Spain · Cellar & vineyard · moderate

  43. A pintxo crawl, done properly

    One drink and one signature dish per bar, standing, then move. Sitting down is how visitors get it wrong.

    San Sebastián, Spain · Food & table · moderate

  44. Paella where the rice grows

    The Albufera lagoon south of the city grows the rice and cooks it over orange wood; lunchtime only, and never in the evening.

    Valencia, Spain · Food & table · moderate

  45. A proper fika circuit

    Four bakeries, one afternoon, and the argument about whether the cardamom bun beats the cinnamon one.

    Stockholm, Sweden · Food & table · low

  46. A two-hour Turkish breakfast

    Fifteen small dishes, endless tea, and no expectation that you leave. Order for the table and clear the morning.

    Istanbul, Türkiye · Food & table · low

  47. Up Arthur's Seat before breakfast

    251 metres of extinct volcano, forty minutes up, and the whole city and the Forth laid out at the top.

    Edinburgh, United Kingdom · Walk or hike · low

  48. Three distilleries on foot

    Ardbeg, Lagavulin and Laphroaig sit within five kilometres of each other along one coastal path — walk it, for obvious reasons.

    Islay, United Kingdom · Cellar & vineyard · moderate

How this list is built: Everything we classified as table, cellar, plus anything whose own name or description matches one of the 41 terms under the sub-categories below. Both tests are about the experience itself. Neither the name of the town nor the interests the town is tagged with can put something in this list: a salt mine in Hallstatt is not a market, and a bunker museum in a city known for its food is not a meal.
