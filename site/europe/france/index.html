Western Europe

France

The most visited country on earth, and still mostly empty fifty kilometres from anywhere famous.

France is the size of Texas with every landscape Europe offers: Atlantic surf, alpine glacier, Mediterranean cliff, volcanic upland and four hundred kilometres of vineyard between them. It receives more visitors than any country in the world, and almost all of them go to the same six places — which leaves the Auvergne, the Jura, the Aveyron and half of Brittany to whoever bothers. Food and wine are not a sector here; they are the organising principle.

Getting around

The TGV network makes Paris–Marseille three hours and Paris–Bordeaux two. Book on the day fares open and pay a third. Rural France needs a car; the regional TER trains are slow, cheap and lovely if you have the time.

When to come

Paris in October is better than Paris in July: warm enough, half the queue, and the light is famously kinder. The whole country goes on holiday in August and the cities empty while the coast fills. September is harvest in the vineyards, which is the best and busiest time to be in them.

Worth knowing

  • Lunch is 12 to 2 and a rural kitchen closes at 13:45 regardless of your plans.
  • Bonjour on entering a shop is not optional politeness; skipping it reads as rudeness.
  • Many museums are free on the first Sunday of the month, and unbearable on it.

At the table

  • Bread bought twice a day, because it is worth nothing by evening
  • Cheese as a course, in order, before dessert
  • The regional dish that is not exported: cassoulet, choucroute, aligot, socca — each belongs to one place
