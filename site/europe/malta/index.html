The portrait

Why Malta belongs in your Europe.

Malta is 316 square kilometres, the most densely populated country in the EU, and has been fought over by everyone with a navy since the Bronze Age. Its megalithic temples predate Stonehenge and Giza. Valletta was built from scratch in the 1560s as a fortress city and is still walkable in twenty minutes. English is an official language, which makes it unusually easy going.

Where Malta is worth going

4 destinations across 2 travel regions.

Getting around

Buses reach everywhere slowly and cheaply from Valletta. Ferries link Valletta, Sliema and the Three Cities, and Ċirkewwa to Gozo. Distances are tiny; traffic is not.

When to come

July and August are very hot and very full. Spring is green and brief — the island is brown by June. Winter is mild, swimmable on warm days, and almost empty.

Worth knowing

  • Driving is on the left, a British legacy along with the letterboxes.
  • The temples require timed tickets and Ħaġar Qim is roofed for protection.
  • Swimming is off rocks and platforms more often than sand; the Blue Lagoon is beautiful and mobbed.

At the table

  • Rabbit stew (fenkata), the national meal, eaten in company
  • Pastizzi — flaky pastry with ricotta or peas, from a hole in the wall
  • Ftira: sourdough with tuna, capers and tomato, and a UNESCO listing
