The Baltic States

Lithuania

A baroque capital, a sand spit shared with Russia, and a hill of a hundred thousand crosses.

Lithuania was once the largest state in Europe, and the memory of it sits oddly on a country of under three million. Vilnius has the biggest baroque old town in the region, full of courtyards you walk into by accident. The Curonian Spit is a 98-kilometre dune of shifting sand, and the Hill of Crosses is one of the strangest and most moving places on the continent.

Getting around

Vilnius, Kaunas and Klaipėda are linked by frequent buses; the coast needs a ferry from Klaipėda to the spit. The old town is entirely walkable and mostly cobbled.

When to come

Summer for the coast and the long evenings. September is warm, cheap and empty. Vilnius is at its best in the first week of March, when the Kaziukas craft fair takes over the old town.

Worth knowing

  • The Hill of Crosses is an active devotional site, not a photo opportunity — leave one or leave it alone.
  • Užupis, in Vilnius, declared itself a republic in 1997 and has a constitution on a wall in twenty languages.
  • The Curonian Spit is shared with the Russian exclave of Kaliningrad; the border is real and closed to casual crossing.

At the table

  • Cepelinai: potato dumplings the size and shape of a zeppelin
  • Cold beetroot soup, shocking pink, served with a hot potato
  • Dark rye bread with garlic, fried, eaten with beer
