Belarus

Half forest, with the last primeval woodland in Europe and a capital rebuilt entirely in Stalinist stone.

Check government travel advice before planning anything here

Many European governments advise against all or all but essential travel to Belarus, citing the security situation and the risk of arbitrary detention. This page records the country; it does not recommend visiting it. Check your own government's current advice.

EuropeDoor keeps a page for every country in Europe, including the ones nobody should be travelling to right now. A page here is a record, not a recommendation, and countries at this level are excluded from the Journey Planner.

Belarus is flat, forested and thinly populated, with the Belarusian half of Białowieża — the last primeval lowland forest on the continent — along its southwestern border. Minsk was almost completely destroyed in the Second World War and rebuilt as a planned socialist city, which makes it one of the most coherent examples of that architecture anywhere.

Getting around

Rail and long-distance buses historically linked all regional centres. Current practicalities are governed by border and visa conditions that change often.

When to come

Historically summer for the forests and lakes. This is context rather than a recommendation — see the advisory above.

Worth knowing

  • Check your own government's current travel advice before considering any journey.
  • This country is excluded from the Journey Planner.
  • Border crossings and visa arrangements have changed repeatedly and current information ages fast.
