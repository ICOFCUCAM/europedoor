Budapest & the Danube Bend, Hungary

Budapest

Hilly Buda and flat Pest, joined by bridges, with a parliament on the water and a hundred hot springs underneath.

Why visit

  • Széchenyi and Gellért baths — one grand and outdoor, one Art Nouveau and indoor
  • The parliament, seen from the Buda side at night
  • The Great Market Hall, and lángos on the upper floor
  • The House of Terror, in the building both secret polices used

Places to see

4 recorded so far. We hold what each one is and how long to give it, and deliberately not its opening hours or price.

Széchenyi Baths

Eighteen pools fed from a well 1,246 m down. The outdoor pools stay at 38°C, which is why the photographs of people playing chess in the steam are all taken in winter.

Baths · 2–3 hours

Fisherman's Bastion

Seven neo-Romanesque turrets for the seven Magyar tribes, built in 1902 purely as a viewing terrace. The upper levels charge; the lower ones are free and nearly as good.

Viewpoint · 1 hour

Great Market Hall

Three floors under a Zsolnay tiled roof: paprika and salami on the ground, cooked food and a great deal of embroidery above.

Market · 1–2 hours

House of Terror

The headquarters of first the Arrow Cross and then the state security police, kept as a museum of both occupations. The basement cells are as they were found.

Museum · 2 hours

Travel tips

Practical things about Hungary that are not obvious from outside it.

  • The forint, not the euro; exchange rates at street bureaux are usually terrible.
  • In the baths, bring flip-flops and a towel or hire both; some pools require a cap.
  • Ruin bars occupy derelict courtyards in the old Jewish quarter, which is also a memorial district — the two coexist awkwardly.
