Ireland

A small wet island that exported its writers, its music and a third of its people, and kept the landscape.

Ireland is green because it rains, and the rain arrives from the Atlantic in bands with sun between them. The west is the draw: limestone pavement, sea cliffs, a language still spoken daily, and traditional music played in pubs for the players rather than the audience. Dublin is small for a capital and dense with literary ghosts.

Getting around

Dublin to Galway and Cork by train is easy; everything else really wants a car, and the small roads are small. Buses reach the peninsulas slowly. Ferries serve the Aran Islands from Rossaveel and Doolin.

When to come

May and September give the best odds of dry days and the roads are passable. Summer is busy on the Wild Atlantic Way and accommodation in Kerry books out months ahead. Winter is dark, cheap and atmospheric if you want fires and sessions.

Worth knowing

  • Ireland is in the EU but not Schengen; the Common Travel Area with the UK works separately.
  • Traditional sessions are for the musicians — sit, listen, and do not clap over the tune's end.
  • Rural addresses often have no street numbers; Eircodes work where satnav does not.

At the table

  • Brown soda bread with butter and smoked salmon
  • Oysters from Galway Bay, September onwards
  • Seafood chowder, which is a meal and a test of a pub
