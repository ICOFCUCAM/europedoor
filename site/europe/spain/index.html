The portrait

Why Spain belongs in your Europe.

Spain is a high dry plateau with green mountains at the top and a coastline everyone else visits. It carries eight centuries of Islamic rule in its south, a Celtic wet north, and languages that are not dialects of anything. Eating happens late — lunch at two, dinner at ten — and the pace only makes sense once you accept it. The interior, Extremadura, Aragón, Galicia’s inland, is nearly empty.

Getting around

AVE high-speed rail links Madrid to Barcelona, Seville, Valencia and Málaga in two to three hours; book early. Buses cover the interior where rail does not. Northern Spain needs a car.

When to come

July and August in the interior are brutal — Córdoba regularly passes 44°C — and the coast is full. May, June, September and October are the real seasons. The green north is best in summer, when everywhere else is unbearable.

Worth knowing

  • Lunch runs 14:00–16:00 and kitchens genuinely close; dinner starts at 21:00.
  • Many museums are free in the last two hours of the day.
  • The Camino de Santiago is a working pilgrimage with its own etiquette, not a long-distance hiking route.

At the table

  • Tapas, which is a way of eating rather than a dish — and free with a drink in Granada and León
  • Jamón ibérico de bellota, from pigs that ate acorns, sliced by hand
  • Regional: paella only in Valencia, pintxos in the Basque country, pulpo in Galicia

The grid, read as a plan
