Lisbon & the West, Portugal

Lisbon

A tiled, hilly, sea-facing capital rebuilt on a grid after the 1755 earthquake, with trams that still climb the old parts.

Why visit

  • The Gulbenkian, one of the best private collections ever assembled, in a garden
  • Jerónimos monastery's Manueline cloister at Belém
  • Tram 28 at seven in the morning, or not at all
  • Fado in Alfama, in a room of thirty people

Travel tips

Practical things about Portugal that are not obvious from outside it.

  • The couvert — bread, olives, cheese put on your table — is charged unless you send it back.
  • Fado has two schools, Lisbon's and Coimbra's, and neither is background music.
  • Atlantic beaches have real currents; the flag system is enforced and worth obeying.
