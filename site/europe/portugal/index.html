The Mediterranean

Portugal

Europe's western edge, facing an ocean it once mapped, with the oldest fixed borders on the continent.

Portugal has looked outward for six hundred years and inward for the rest, which shows in its architecture — Manueline stonework covered in ropes and sea creatures — and its food, built on cod fished off Newfoundland. The Atlantic keeps it cooler and wilder than Spain. Lisbon and Porto have both been thoroughly discovered; the Alentejo, the Beiras and the interior have not.

Getting around

Alfa Pendular trains link Lisbon and Porto in under three hours. The Douro line is one of Europe's great scenic railways. Buses reach the Alentejo; a car helps everywhere rural.

When to come

Spring and autumn are the best of it; the Algarve is full from June to September and the interior is very hot. The Douro harvest in late September is the country at its most itself. Winter is mild, wet and cheap.

Worth knowing

  • The couvert — bread, olives, cheese put on your table — is charged unless you send it back.
  • Fado has two schools, Lisbon's and Coimbra's, and neither is background music.
  • Atlantic beaches have real currents; the flag system is enforced and worth obeying.

At the table

  • Bacalhau, salt cod, in a claimed 365 preparations
  • Pastel de nata, eaten warm with cinnamon, standing at a counter
  • Grilled sardines in June, and the whole country smells of them
