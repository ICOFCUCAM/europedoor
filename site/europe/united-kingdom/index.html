Britain & Ireland

United Kingdom

Four nations, one small island group, and an argument about all of it that has run for a thousand years.

The UK compresses an enormous amount into a short train ride: a global city, a Roman spa town, a mining valley, a Hebridean beach and a border wall built by an emperor, all within a day of each other. Nothing is far and everything is different — the accent changes every forty miles and so does the beer. It left the EU in 2020, so entry, roaming and customs work differently from its neighbours.

Getting around

Rail reaches almost everywhere and costs least when booked weeks ahead; advance singles are a different price from walk-up fares by a factor of four. Scotland's islands run on CalMac ferries. London's Oyster/contactless system also covers most of the south-east.

When to come

May and September are the sweet spot: long days, functioning gardens, no school holidays. Scotland's midges rule out still evenings in July and August on the west coast. Nowhere is reliably dry and nobody plans around it.

Worth knowing

  • Not in Schengen: your Schengen days do not apply here and the border check is real.
  • Driving is on the left, and rural roads in Wales and the Highlands are single-track with passing places.
  • Right to roam exists in Scotland and barely exists in England — footpaths there are rights of way across private land, which is a different thing.

At the table

  • A Sunday roast, which is a meal and an institution
  • Indian food, in the specifically British forms invented here
  • Cheese: over 700 named varieties, most of them regional and unexported
