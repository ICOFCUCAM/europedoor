The Adriatic & Balkans

Serbia

A confluence capital with the best nightlife in the Balkans, and monasteries frescoed eight hundred years ago.

Serbia sits on the Danube where central Europe stops being central Europe. Belgrade has been destroyed and rebuilt dozens of times and behaves accordingly — unsentimental, loud and very good company. The north, Vojvodina, is flat, Hungarian-influenced and full of paprika; the south and west hold medieval monasteries with frescoes among the finest in Orthodox Europe.

Getting around

Buses cover the country densely; the Belgrade–Novi Sad fast railway opened recently. The Šargan Eight narrow-gauge line in the west is a ride for its own sake.

When to come

Late spring and early autumn are best; Belgrade in July is hot and the river barges are the only sensible place to be. Winter is cold and the trumpet festival at Guča in August is a phenomenon in its own right.

Worth knowing

  • Not in the EU; the dinar is the currency and cash is more useful outside cities.
  • Positions on Kosovo are politically charged here — visitors are best served by listening rather than opining.
  • Smoking indoors is more common than elsewhere in Europe.

At the table

  • Ćevapi and pljeskavica, grilled and enormous
  • Ajvar, roast red pepper relish, made at home each autumn
  • Rakija, fruit brandy, offered on arrival and refusing it is difficult
