Bergen

A hanseatic trading port wedged between seven mountains, wet 200 days a year and entirely unbothered about it.

Places to see

Bryggen

The leaning wooden trading houses of the Hanseatic wharf, rebuilt after each fire on the same foundations since the 1100s.

Quarter · 1 hour

Fløyen

320 metres above the city by funicular, with a path down through the woods that most people forget to take.

Viewpoint · 2 hours

Fish Market

Outdoor from May, indoors year-round, and better one street back where the boats actually deliver.

Market · 45 minutes

KODE

Four buildings on one street holding Munch, Astrup and the largest Norwegian art collection outside Oslo.

Museum · 2 hours

Travel tips

Practical things about Norway that are not obvious from outside it.

  • Card and phone payment work everywhere, including on ferries and in mountain huts. Cash is close to useless.
  • Allemannsretten — the right to roam — lets you walk and camp on uncultivated land, with obligations attached: no fires in summer, no trace, 150 metres from any house.
  • Alcohol above 4.7% is sold only in state Vinmonopolet shops, which close early and shut on Sundays.
