Fjord Norway, Norway

Stavanger

Oil capital with a preserved wooden old town, and the most photographed cliff in Norway an hour away.

≈ Coast & beaches⚑ History & ruins● Food▲ Mountains

Why visit

  • Gamle Stavanger: 173 white wooden houses from the 18th century
  • Preikestolen, a 604-metre flat cliff, four hours there and back
  • The Norwegian Petroleum Museum, which is franker about the industry than you expect

Places to see

3 recorded so far. We hold what each one is and how long to give it, and deliberately not its opening hours or price.

Preikestolen

A flat slab 604 metres above the Lysefjord with no railing, reached by an eight-kilometre path.

Mountain · 4–5 hours

Gamle Stavanger

173 white wooden houses from the 18th and 19th centuries, on cobbled lanes above the harbour.

Quarter · 45 minutes

Norwegian Petroleum Museum

The industry that paid for modern Norway, explained more frankly than a national museum usually manages.

Museum · 2 hours

Things to do

Preikestolen, early

Eight kilometres round on rock and boardwalk. Start before seven; by eleven there is a queue for the photograph and no railing to queue behind.

Walk or hike · low
