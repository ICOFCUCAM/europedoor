The portrait

Why Norway belongs in your Europe.

Norway is long — its coastline, unfolded, would wrap a good way round the world — and almost everything worth seeing sits where rock meets salt water. The interior is high plateau and slow trains; the coast is fjord, ferry and fishing town. Prices are the highest in Europe and the light is the strangest: midnight sun above the Arctic Circle in June, four-hour days in December.

Getting around

The Bergen and Dovre railways are two of Europe’s great train rides and cost less than the equivalent flight if booked early. Coastal Norway runs on ferries and express boats — treat them as public transport, not excursions. North of Trondheim, distances get serious: fly, or take the coastal ferry and give it days rather than hours.

When to come

June and July give you the midnight sun and open mountain roads. Late September brings colour and empty trails. Late January to March is the northern lights and ski season; November is neither.

Worth knowing

  • Card and phone payment work everywhere, including on ferries and in mountain huts. Cash is close to useless.
  • Allemannsretten — the right to roam — lets you walk and camp on uncultivated land, with obligations attached: no fires in summer, no trace, 150 metres from any house.
  • Alcohol above 4.7% is sold only in state Vinmonopolet shops, which close early and shut on Sundays.

At the table

  • Brown cheese (brunost) — caramelised whey, sliced thin, an acquired and worthwhile taste
  • Cured and smoked salmon, and the fresher, less-famous cod
  • Kanelbolle: the cinnamon bun that fuels every hike

A sample of what is listed across Norway.

The commuter ferry as a day out

Ride the public boats to Hovedøya and Gressholmen with a swim towel and a bakery bag; it is a city transport ticket, not a cruise.

Oslo · On the water

Waterfront architecture walk

Opera roof to Munch to the library at Bjørvika — twenty years of a city deciding what it wanted to look like, in ninety minutes.

Oslo · Walk or hike

Maihaugen open-air museum

Two hundred buildings moved log by log from across Gudbrandsdalen; the point is the carpentry, not the costumes.

Lillehammer · Museum or collection

The free ferry, all day

Fredrikstad runs its river crossings free of charge as ordinary transport; ride them between the old town and the new for as long as you like.

Fredrikstad · On the water

Vemork and the heavy water raid

The plant, the pipeline the saboteurs climbed, and an account that does not tidy up how close it came to failing.

Rjukan · Museum or collection

The wooden boat festival

Every August the harbour fills with clinker-built boats and the people who maintain them; go for the sound of the hulls rather than the programme.

Risør · On the water

Fløyen to Ulriken ridge walk

Five hours along the tops between the two cable cars, with the city on one side and nothing but bog and rock on the other.

Bergen · Walk or hike

Lunch at a working fish counter

Skip the tourist stalls; eat where the boats deliver, standing up, whatever came in that morning.
