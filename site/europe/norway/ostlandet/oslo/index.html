Østlandet & the East, Norway

Oslo

A capital that spent its oil money on architecture and then put a forest and a fjord within a tram ride of the result.

◈ Art & museums⌂ Architecture◧ Design & making▦ Big cities♣ Wild nature

Why visit

  • The Munch museum and the National Museum, which between them hold the versions of The Scream worth arguing about
  • The Fram and Kon-Tiki polar ships, kept whole in Bygdøy
  • Island-hopping the inner Oslofjord on a standard public ferry ticket
  • Nordmarka: 1,700 km of forest trail, reached by metro

Places to see

5 recorded so far. We hold what each one is and how long to give it, and deliberately not its opening hours or price.

MUNCH

Thirteen floors on the waterfront holding the artist's own bequest — 26,000 works, including the versions of The Scream worth arguing about.

Museum · 2–3 hours

Fram Museum

The polar ship Fram kept whole indoors, with her hull open so you can walk the decks that drifted across the Arctic for three years.

Museum · 90 minutes

Vigeland Sculpture Park

212 bronze and granite figures by one sculptor in one park, free and open at every hour, including four in the morning.

Park · 1–2 hours

Akershus Fortress

A medieval castle on the harbour that was never taken by siege, with the government quarter and the fjord on either side of it.

Castle · 1 hour

Oslo Opera House

A roof that comes down to the water so the public can walk up it, which is the building's actual argument.

Monument · 30 minutes

Things to do

The commuter ferry as a day out

Ride the public boats to Hovedøya and Gressholmen with a swim towel and a bakery bag; it is a city transport ticket, not a cruise.

On the water · low

Waterfront architecture walk

Opera roof to Munch to the library at Bjørvika — twenty years of a city deciding what it wanted to look like, in ninety minutes.

Walk or hike · low

Travel tips

Practical things about Norway that are not obvious from outside it.

  • Card and phone payment work everywhere, including on ferries and in mountain huts. Cash is close to useless.
  • Allemannsretten — the right to roam — lets you walk and camp on uncultivated land, with obligations attached: no fires in summer, no trace, 150 metres from any house.
  • Alcohol above 4.7% is sold only in state Vinmonopolet shops, which close early and shut on Sundays.
