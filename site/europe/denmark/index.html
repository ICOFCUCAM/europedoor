The Nordics

Denmark

Small, flat, bicycled, and disproportionately good at deciding how things should look.

Denmark is a peninsula and four hundred islands, none of them far from the sea and none of them high. What it lacks in landscape it returns in design, food and a kind of practised ease — the country invented a word for it and then exported the word. Everything is close: you can cross the whole country by train in four hours.

Getting around

The train network is dense and the bridges are spectacular — the Great Belt and the Øresund both. Copenhagen is genuinely faster by bicycle than by car, and rental is everywhere. Ferries reach the islands the bridges skipped.

When to come

Summer is long-evening weather, and the whole country moves outdoors. April and May are bright and cold. Danish winter is not dark in the Norwegian sense, but it is grey for four months and the country compensates with candles.

Worth knowing

  • Cash is nearly extinct; MobilePay and cards run the country.
  • Cycle lanes have their own rules and Danes enforce them by glare — signal, and keep right.
  • Almost everything closes on Sundays outside the big cities.

At the table

  • Smørrebrød: open sandwiches taken seriously, with rules about order
  • New Nordic cooking, which started here and changed restaurants everywhere
  • A hot dog from a pølsevogn at two in the morning, remorselessly
