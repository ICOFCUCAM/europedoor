The Nordics

Iceland

A geological work in progress, with a literary tradition older than most European states.

Iceland sits on the seam between two continental plates, which is why it steams, erupts and rearranges itself. Three hundred and eighty thousand people live around the edge; the interior is uninhabited and closed by snow for most of the year. The sagas were written here in the 13th century and the language has changed so little that schoolchildren still read them.

Getting around

Ring Road 1 circles the island in 1,330 km and most visitors drive it. F-roads into the interior need a genuine 4x4 and are closed until late June. Check road.is and vedur.is every morning; Icelanders do.

When to come

June and July give near-permanent daylight and open highland roads. September is cheaper, emptier, and the aurora returns. Winter is workable on the south coast and unwise in the interior — the weather changes faster than the forecast.

Worth knowing

  • Weather closes roads with little warning; a rental booked in July can still be stuck in a storm.
  • Off-road driving is illegal and damages moss that takes decades to regrow.
  • Tap water is glacial and free; buying it bottled is the most conspicuous tourist error in the country.

At the table

  • Lamb that has spent the summer eating wild herbs on a mountain
  • Skyr, which is a cheese pretending to be a yoghurt
  • Hot dogs with crispy onions, defensible at any hour
