The portrait

Why Cyprus belongs in your Europe.

Cyprus is the third-largest Mediterranean island and has been divided since 1974, with a UN buffer zone running through the middle of Nicosia. The Republic controls the south; the north is administered separately and recognised only by Türkiye. Beyond the politics, it holds painted Byzantine churches in the Troodos mountains, Roman mosaics at Paphos, and one of the oldest wine traditions in the world.

Where Cyprus is worth going

3 destinations across 2 travel regions.

Getting around

No railway at all; buses connect the cities and a car is close to essential for the mountains. Crossing to the north on foot at Ledra Street in Nicosia is straightforward for most visitors with a passport, subject to current rules.

When to come

Spring is the island’s best season — green, flowering and warm enough to swim by May. July and August are extremely hot inland. The Troodos mountains hold snow and a small ski area in February.

Worth knowing

  • Driving is on the left.
  • Crossing points between south and north exist and rules change — check current guidance before planning around one.
  • The Troodos churches are small, locked and often opened by a neighbour with a key; a donation is expected.

At the table

  • Meze, twenty or more small plates, ordered for the table and never finished
  • Halloumi, grilled, which is a protected local product and tastes different here
  • Commandaria: a sweet wine made the same way since antiquity
