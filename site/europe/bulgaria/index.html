Bulgaria

Thracian gold, Orthodox monasteries, rose fields and the cheapest good skiing in Europe.

5 destinations across 2 regions · capital Sofia

Bulgaria has been continuously inhabited since the Neolithic and holds the oldest processed gold in the world, from a grave at Varna. It is mountainous — two ranges cross it — with a Black Sea coast, a valley that supplies most of the world's rose oil, and a monastery tradition that kept the language alive under five centuries of Ottoman rule. It is one of the best-value countries in the EU.

Getting around

Buses beat the slow railways for most journeys, though the narrow-gauge Rhodope line is worth taking for itself. Sofia has a metro. A car reaches the monasteries and the mountain villages.

When to come

The Rila and Pirin mountains are walkable July to September. The rose harvest is a few weeks in late May and early June. Winter skiing at Bansko is genuinely cheap. The Black Sea coast is full in August.

Worth knowing

  • Shaking the head means yes and nodding can mean no — it catches everyone out.
  • In the EU but the lev is still the currency, pegged to the euro.
  • Mountain huts in Rila and Pirin are basic, cheap and often full at weekends.

At the table

  • Shopska salad, with white brine cheese grated over the top
  • Banitsa, a filo and cheese pastry, for breakfast with ayran
  • Wine from Melnik and the Thracian valley, made here for three thousand years
