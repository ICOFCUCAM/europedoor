The portrait

Why Greece belongs in your Europe.

Greece is more mountain than island by area, and the mainland — Epirus, the Pindus, the Peloponnese — is emptier and greener than the postcards suggest. The islands divide into groups with different architecture, food and weather; the Cyclades are white and windy, the Ionians green, the Dodecanese medieval. Everything closes down in the afternoon heat and reopens at six, which is not laziness but sense.

Where Greece is worth going

25 destinations across 3 travel regions.

Getting around

Ferries are the network: fast catamarans and slow open-deck boats, both booked online and both cancelled by wind. Domestic flights save a day to the far islands. The mainland needs a car; the Athens metro is excellent and has an archaeological museum inside a station.

When to come

May, June, September and early October are the sweet spot: swimmable sea, open tavernas, tolerable ruins. August is the whole of Greece and half of Europe on the same ferries. Many island businesses close entirely from November to April.

Worth knowing

  • Ferry cancellations for wind are common and not negotiable; leave a buffer day before a flight home.
  • Many older buildings ask you to bin paper rather than flush it — follow the sign.
  • Archaeological sites close early in winter and midday shade is scarce; go at opening.

At the table

  • Whatever is in the pot that day — ask to see the kitchen, which is normal
  • Greek salad with no lettuce, and bread to finish the oil
  • Wine from Santorini’s assyrtiko and Naoussa’s xinomavro, both serious and both cheap here

The Acropolis Museum, third floor

Sit in the top row and have someone whisper on the orchestra floor. Built in the 4th century BC and still unexplained in full.

The sacred way at opening
