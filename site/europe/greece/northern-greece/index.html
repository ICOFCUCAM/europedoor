Northern Greece

Meteora’s monasteries on rock pillars, Epirus’s stone villages, and Thessaloniki’s food.

6 destinations · 6 places recorded · about 12 nights to see it all

✝ Sacred places▲ Mountains● Food⚑ History & ruins

What Northern Greece is made of

All six of them, the same set the drawing above plots.

Destinations

Meteora (Kalambaka)

Six working monasteries on top of sandstone pillars, reached for centuries by rope ladder and net.

Historic site
1–2 nights

Thessaloniki

Greece’s second city: Byzantine, Ottoman and Sephardic layers, and the country’s most confident food.

City
2–3 nights

Zagori (Monodendri)

Forty-six stone villages in the Pindus mountains, linked by packhorse bridges over the deepest gorge in the world by ratio.

Village
2–3 nights

Pelion (Makrinitsa)

Stone villages with slate roofs on a wooded mountain that drops to beaches on both sides.

Village
2–3 nights

Kavala

An Ottoman aqueduct over a harbour town, with Philippi’s ruins inland and Thasos across the water.

City
1–2 nights

Ioannina

An Ottoman lake city with an island in it, a silversmithing tradition and the gateway to Zagori.

City
1–2 nights

Great Meteoron

Varlaam

Frescoed throughout, with the windlass tower that hauled everything up for four hundred years.

Meteora (Kalambaka) · Monastery

The Rotunda

A 4th-century Roman rotunda that has been a mausoleum, a church and a mosque, and keeps its minaret.

Jewish Museum of Thessaloniki

Four centuries of a majority-Sephardic city, and 1943, told without flinching.

Thessaloniki · Museum

Vikos Gorge

The deepest gorge in the world by width-to-depth ratio, seen from Oxya or walked from inside.
