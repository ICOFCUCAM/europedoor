Alpine & Central Europe

Slovenia

Alps, karst caves, vineyards and 46 kilometres of coast, all within two hours of each other.

Slovenia is the size of Wales and contains a proper alpine range, one of the world's great cave systems, Mediterranean coast, and more forest cover than almost any country in Europe. It has been quietly excellent at sustainable tourism for a decade and the capital pedestrianised its centre in 2007 without much fuss. Everything is close, which is the whole point.

Getting around

Distances are tiny — Ljubljana to the coast is 90 minutes, to the Alps one hour. Buses beat trains for the mountains. A car helps in the Soča valley and the Karst.

When to come

June and September are ideal: the high trails are open, the lakes are swimmable and Bled is not solid. Winter gives cheap alpine skiing and thermal spas in the east.

Worth knowing

  • A vignette is required for motorways and checked automatically.
  • Cave temperatures sit around 8°C year-round; bring a layer even in August.
  • The Julian Alps are limestone — via ferrata is common and needs real equipment.

At the table

  • Potica: a rolled nut cake, at every celebration
  • Štruklji, dumplings rolled with anything from tarragon to cottage cheese
  • Wine from three distinct regions, almost none of it exported
