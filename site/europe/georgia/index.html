The portrait

Why Georgia belongs in your Europe.

Georgia has been making wine in buried clay qvevri for eight millennia — the oldest evidence anywhere — and has an alphabet used by no other language. It sits on the southern side of the Greater Caucasus, with 5,000-metre peaks, and its supra feasting tradition, presided over by a tamada, is a formal institution rather than a party. A Council of Europe member, and European by most political definitions.

Where Georgia is worth going

4 destinations across 2 travel regions.

Getting around

Marshrutka minibuses reach everywhere, leaving when full. The Tbilisi–Batumi train is comfortable. Mountain roads need a 4x4 and a driver who knows them; the Tusheti road is genuinely dangerous.

When to come

Late spring and early autumn are best; the harvest (rtveli) in September and October is the country at its most characteristic. High mountain roads to Tusheti and Ushguli open roughly June to October and close hard.

Worth knowing

  • A supra has rules: the tamada leads, toasts are made in sequence, and you do not toast out of turn.
  • Abkhazia and South Ossetia are outside government control and should not be entered from Russia.
  • Sulphur baths in Tbilisi are a genuine local habit, not a tourist product.

At the table

  • Khachapuri, cheese bread, in regional forms — the Adjarian one has an egg on top
  • Khinkali, soup dumplings held by the twisted top, which is left uneaten
  • Amber wine: white grapes fermented on skins in buried qvevri
