Prague & Bohemia, Czechia

Prague

Romanesque, gothic, baroque and cubist stacked on top of each other, with a castle above and a bridge in between.

Why visit

  • Charles Bridge at six in the morning — genuinely worth the alarm
  • The castle complex and St Vitus's stained glass by Mucha
  • Vyšehrad, the other castle, with almost nobody on it
  • A pub where they draw a mlíko, a glass of pure foam, and mean it

Places to see

4 recorded so far. We hold what each one is and how long to give it, and deliberately not its opening hours or price.

Charles Bridge

Fourteenth-century, thirty baroque statues, and so crowded by ten in the morning that the only honest advice is to walk it at dawn or after midnight.

Bridge · 45 minutes

Prague Castle

Not a castle so much as a walled hill town that has been the seat of Bohemian kings, Holy Roman emperors and Czech presidents in turn. St Vitus Cathedral is inside it.

Castle · 3–4 hours

Josefov, the Jewish Quarter

Six synagogues and a cemetery where twelve thousand headstones lean against each other because the ground was reused for three hundred years and there was nowhere else to bury.

Quarter · 3 hours

Vyšehrad and the river bend

The other castle rock, south along the Vltava, with a neo-gothic basilica and the national cemetery. Locals come here to avoid the crowds at the first one.

Ruin · 2 hours

Travel tips

Practical things about Czechia that are not obvious from outside it.

  • Beer is often cheaper than water in a pub and ordering is done by putting a mat on the table.
  • The koruna, not the euro; card acceptance is near-universal in cities.
  • Prague's centre has aggressive currency-exchange scams — use a bank or an ATM from a real bank.
