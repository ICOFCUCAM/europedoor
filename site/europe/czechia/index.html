Alpine & Central Europe

Czechia

The most beautiful city in central Europe, and a country that drinks more beer per head than any other.

Bohemia and Moravia were the industrial heart of the Habsburg empire and came through the twentieth century with their architecture largely intact — Prague was not bombed. Outside it, spa towns, sandstone rock cities, Moravian vineyards and a chateau in every second valley go almost unvisited. Beer is a food group here and has been brewed in Plzeň since 1842.

Getting around

Trains and buses are cheap, frequent and comfortable; RegioJet and České dráhy both serve the main routes. Prague's metro and trams are excellent and the night trams run all night.

When to come

Prague is busy from April to October and at Christmas. May and September are the best compromise. Winter is cold, cheap and atmospheric, and the beer halls are the point.

Worth knowing

  • Beer is often cheaper than water in a pub and ordering is done by putting a mat on the table.
  • The koruna, not the euro; card acceptance is near-universal in cities.
  • Prague's centre has aggressive currency-exchange scams — use a bank or an ATM from a real bank.

At the table

  • Roast pork, dumplings and cabbage — vepřo knedlo zelo, the national plate
  • Svíčková: beef in a cream root-vegetable sauce with cranberry
  • Pilsner, drawn slowly with a thick head, at 4°C
