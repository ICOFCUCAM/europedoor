Andorra

A Pyrenean co-principality with two heads of state, no airport, and the highest capital in Europe.

2 destinations across 1 region · capital Andorra la Vella

▲ Mountains❄ Snow & winter♣ Wild nature✝ Sacred places

Andorra is 468 square kilometres of Pyrenean valley, jointly headed by the President of France and a Spanish bishop — an arrangement running since 1278. Catalan is the official language. It is best known for duty-free shopping and skiing, and least known for Romanesque chapels and a genuinely wild high valley on the UNESCO list.

Getting around

No airport and no railway. Buses run from Barcelona, Toulouse and the French rail station at L'Hospitalet. Inside the country, buses go up every valley.

When to come

Two clear seasons: skiing from December to April, and high walking from late June to September. May and October are between them and much of the mountain infrastructure shuts.

Worth knowing

  • Not in the EU or Schengen, but there are no routine border controls with France or Spain; customs limits are enforced.
  • Fuel, alcohol and electronics are cheaper here, which is why the main road is a queue on Saturdays.
  • The Madriu-Perafita-Claror valley has no road at all and is entered on foot.

At the table

  • Trinxat: cabbage, potato and bacon, mountain winter food
  • Escudella, a heavy Catalan stew
  • Cheese and charcuterie from the valley farms, and Pyrenean lamb
