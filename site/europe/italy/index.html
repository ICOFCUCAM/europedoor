The portrait

Why Italy belongs in your Europe.

Italy holds more UNESCO World Heritage sites than any other country and treats most of them as ordinary streets. It became one state only in 1861, and the food, dialect and civic pride still change every forty kilometres — the pasta shape tells you where you are. The Alps in the north are as serious as Switzerland’s; the south is Greek, Arab and Norman by turns.

Getting around

Frecciarossa and Italo make Milan–Rome three hours and compete on price. Regional trains are cheap and reach hill towns. Driving into historic centres is restricted by ZTL cameras and the fines arrive months later, abroad.

When to come

April to June and September to October. August is hot, and Italians themselves leave the cities for the coast, closing half the restaurants you wanted. The Dolomites have a separate July-September walking season and a December-March snow one.

Worth knowing

  • Coperto is a per-person cover charge and is legal and normal; a tip on top is not expected.
  • ZTL zones in historic centres are camera-enforced — never drive in without a hotel permit.
  • Churches enforce covered shoulders and knees, including in high summer.

At the table

  • Pasta by region — trofie in Liguria, orecchiette in Puglia, carbonara only in Rome
  • Coffee standing at the bar, and never a cappuccino after breakfast
  • Wine by the region it is drunk in: Barolo north, Chianti centre, Etna south

Testaccio, where Rome eats

The market and the streets around it are where Roman offal cooking survives; go at one, order what the sign says.

Rome · Food & table

The Appian Way on a Sunday

The road is closed to traffic on Sundays: basalt paving from 312 BC, catacombs, aqueducts and countryside, inside the city.

Rome · Walk or hike

Vespers at San Miniato

Gregorian chant in an 11th-century church above the city, at 17:30, free, and almost always uncrowded.

Florence · Sacred site

The 666 arches to San Luca

Four kilometres of covered walkway climbing out of the city, built so a procession would never get wet.

Bologna · Walk or hike

Round the walls on a bicycle

Four kilometres of Renaissance rampart, planted with plane trees in 1818 and used as a promenade ever since.

Lucca · Rail, road or ride

Cicchetti and an ombra

Small plates and a small glass of wine at a bàcaro, standing, moving on after one — the Venetian version of an aperitivo.

Venice · Food & table

A night in a rifugio

Dinner at seven, lights out at ten, and the Enrosadira — the pink glow on the rock at dawn — from the door.

Ortisei & the Dolomites · Walk or hike

Vermouth where it was invented
