# Product

**EuropeDoor — open the door to Europe.**

Discover Europe. Understand its places. Build your journey. Experience it.

## What it is

A European discovery, cultural knowledge and journey-planning platform. Fifty
countries organised the way people actually travel them — by shared coast,
shared mountain range and shared history — down to 319 destinations and the
things worth doing in each, held in one structure so that a fjord in Vestland
and a cellar in Alentejo can appear in the same itinerary without either being
flattened into a listicle.

## The four doors

Discover → Understand → Experience → Journey.

A **sequence, not a menu**: each is only worth anything once the one before it
has happened. That is the argument against Search → Book, and it is the
product's whole shape.

## What makes it different

Not the database. Anyone can import 40,000 places. The differentiators are:

1. **Depth over breadth.** 319 destinations written properly, and no importer
   in the repository on purpose.
2. **The graph, not the hierarchy.** Places know their journeys, stories and
   themes, both ways.
3. **Honesty as a feature.** Four labels on the page, an expiring verification
   record, a public board reading 0 of 50, and a planner that **refuses**
   rather than returning a confident bad itinerary.
4. **Nothing is purchasable.** No ranking field exists, so the promise that
   money cannot move a place up a list is enforced by schema rather than by
   policy.

## Who it is for

Seven traveller types have a route through the product. Two — family and
luxury — are derived by published rule rather than from data we hold, and the
accessibility needs of any of them are **not held at all**, which
`/accessibility` says plainly.

## What it does not do

No booking, no ratings, no reviews, no opening hours, no photographs yet, no
accounts, no analytics, no payments. Each is a decision with a published
reason, not a gap: see `/how-it-works`, which separates *built*, *designed,
not built* and *deliberately blocked*.

The single blocker behind most of that list is that **no operating entity
exists**, which is stated on the site rather than implied.
